1. Parties
The Parties to this Data Sharing Agreement (“DSA”) are:
1.1 THE STATISTICS BOARD acting through the Office for National Statistics, its
executive body, of Statistics House, Tredegar Park, Newport NP10 8XG (“the
ONS”).
1.2 London Borough of Camden 5 Pancras Square London N1C 4AG (“the
Council”)
2. Status
2.1 This DSA is not legally binding and no legal obligations or legal rights shall
arise between the Parties from the provisions of the DSA. By signing this
agreement the parties agree to adhere to the framework set out in this DSA.
3. Purpose
3.1 The purpose of this DSA is to set out the roles and obligations of each of the
Parties.
4. Background
4.1 An ONS feasibility study in 2017 identified the benefits of using Council Tax
data to help meet our transformation goals. As the required data is beyond
what is collected centrally by any government department, ONS are therefore
acquiring Council Tax data from every Local Authority in Great Britain.
4.2 Acquisition of Council Tax data will also move ONS towards achieving its
corporate aims laid out in the Statistics for the Public Good strategy published
in July 2020. The aims of this strategy include, but are not limited to; providing
faster data to support critical decision making on a daily basis, delivering a
successful Census in March 2021 with high-quality outputs from the Census
in 2022 and 2023, and delivering population and migration statistics based on
administration data burden.
4.3 In line with the above corporate aims, this administrative source will also
enable ONS to enhance our current statistical outputs in terms of both quality
and timeliness, and may in the future be used in the production of new
statistical outputs.

5. Legal basis
5.1 Section 45A of the Statistics and Registration Service Act 2007 (as inserted
by section 79 of the Digital Economy Act 2017) permits any public authority to
disclose to the Statistics Board any information they hold in connection with
their functions.
5.2 Subject to the provisions laid out in section 53A of the Statistics and
Registration Service Act 2007 (as inserted by section 81 of the Digital
Economy Act 2017), ONS may disclose information to a devolved authority if
that information is required by the devolved authority for their statistical
functions, only with the consent of the public authority to do so.

7. Data use
7.1 Council Tax data supplied under this agreement will be used by ONS for the
purposes of any one or more of its functions under the Statistics and
Registration Service Act 2007, the data will also be used in accordance with
ONS’ functions laid out in the Census Act 1920.
7.2 Examples of work that will be undertaken by ONS using Council Tax data are
as followed;
• Social Survey Division: Replace Council Tax related questions on four social
surveys (Survey on Living Conditions, Living Costs and Food Survey, Wealth
and Assets Survey, Family Resources Survey), used to produce new statistical
outputs and enhance current statistical outputs. Also replace survey questions
about disability and carers at a Local Authority level to produce more accurate
and granular health related modelling.
• Address Register Team: Creating and enhancing our address register for the
Census.
• Admin Data Census: improve population research outputs by creating an
indicator of activity to use for people and addresses. Contribute to admin-
based population estimates and internal migration statistics.
• Methodology: Efficient sample designs and improve precision of estimators
• Public Policy Division: Improve the statistics about the number of new
properties built. Also help improve dwelling stock estimates by enabling a sub-
national vacancy rate adjustment, which isn't currently possible between
censuses.
• Census Transformation Programme 2021: Help with enumeration,
especially of hard to count population / geographic areas. Quality assurance
and validation of census responses and estimates, improve the accuracy of
census estimates. Council Tax data is vital in helping build pictures for signs
of activity and inactivity, indicators for missed and vacant addresses.
7.3 Information supplied under this agreement may be disclosed under section
53A SRSA 2007 to a devolved authority following a request in writing outlining
the information which is sought and the purposes for which it is sought.
7.3.1 Information may only be disclosed if ONS is satisfied that the
information is necessary for the purposes of the statistical functions of
the devolved authority and that it will not be used for any other purpose,
and with consent from the Council for the disclosure of the information.

10. Security and assurance
10.1 Data security for storage and linkage of the data will be provided with an
assured ONS data analysis environment that includes the following elements
of security control:
• Need To Know applied through user account access and management
• Controlled ingest and export of data into/out from the environment
• Controlled account access using unique credentials based on job role
• Logged and monitored access of user activity within the environment
• Secure build configuration for infrastructure, including cloud services
• Vulnerability tested infrastructure with appropriate remediation and patching
• Compliance checks against security enforcing controls
• Architectural review against standards and best practice
• Staff security cleared to the appropriate level based on their supervised
and/or unsupervised access to sensitive data in accordance with ONS
clearance policies and data access processes
• Education and awareness of environment users covering security policies
and secure working practices
• Operational support processes to securely manage the environment
• Risk assessment to identify security risks and mitigation actions to reduce
this risk.

10.2 The ONS data access process is focused on promoting data integrity by
ensuring use of data is proportional, business requirements are clearly
defined and the Need to Know principles are implemented. In order to be
granted access to record level data, ONS users must provide a project
proposal describing their use of data and justifying their requirements.
Approval for projects is given on a case-by-case basis by the designated
Information Asset Owner (IAO), with input and advice from Security and
Information Management (SaIM). Each business case for access to data by
an individual or by a project team is reviewed to ensure the following criteria
are met:
• Purposes of the proposed work expected outcomes and benefits to
the ONS and the public are clearly outlined;
• Ethical approval for the proposed project/piece of work is in place;
• Justification for use of all Personal and Special Categories of Personal Data
requested as part of the project is in place;
• The role of each user within the proposed project/piece of work is
clearly specified and users are security-cleared to the appropriate level;
• Requirements for access to multiple sensitive datasets for use within a single
project/piece of work are clearly specified;
• Processes are in place to minimise access to personal identifiers by
segregation of duties, where appropriate.
Access to record level data is time-limited for the specified duration of the
project and regular auditing is in place to ensure compliance with Data
Partner agreements and with specific conditions of data access imposed by
IAOs.
10.3 With reasonable notice, periodic written/verbal checks may be conducted by
an authorised employee of the Council to confirm compliance with this DSA.
10.4 ONS will keep a record of any processing of Personal Data and will provide a
copy of such record to the Council on request.
10.5 ONS will not transfer or permit the transfer of the Data to any territory outside
the UK without the prior written consent of the Council.
