RECITALS
The UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GPDR) Art. 28 and section 59 of the Data
Protection Act 2018 require parties to have in place a binding contract where they have a
controller-processor relationship. This requirement of the GDPR and Act applies to all such
relationships in place from 25 May 2018. This MoU is intended to create a set of data processing
terms which the public bodies in this case can adopt to satisfy this requirement.
INTRODUCTION
The ERO has entered into this data processing MoU with the Department for Levelling Up,
Housing and Communities (DLUHC) for the Department to process personal data for the purposes
of processing personal data using the EROP which will cover three services: application for Voter
Authority Certificate, Online Absent Vote Applications and applications to register as an Overseas
Elector.
This MoU sets out the terms, requirements and conditions on which the Department will process
personal data when providing services under the MoU.
The Parties agree to comply with the terms of this MoU in consideration of the payment by each
Party to the other of the sum of one pound (£1), the receipt and sufficiency of which is
acknowledged by each Party.

(i) nature of the data to be protected.
(ii) harm that might result from a Personal Data Breach.
(iii) state of technological development; and
(iv) cost of implementing any measures;
(c) ensure that:
(i) the Processor Personnel do not process Personal Data except in accordance
with this MoU (and in particular Annex 1);
(ii) it takes all reasonable steps to ensure the reliability and integrity of any
Processor Personnel who have access to the Personal Data and ensure that
they:
(A) are aware of and comply with the Processor’s duties under this clause;
(B) are subject to appropriate confidentiality undertakings with the
Processor or any Sub-processor;

6. ESCALATION PROCEDURE
6.1 If either party has any issues, concerns or complaints about the MoU, or any matter in this
MoU, that Party shall notify the other Party and the parties shall, acting in good faith, seek to
resolve the issue by negotiations between themselves. If the issue cannot be resolved within
7 days, the matter shall be escalated to the ERO and SRO for DLUHC.
6.2 In the event that the escalation procedure in 6.1 does not resolve the issue it shall be finally
resolved by the respective Heads of Organisation, who may also consult with their Data
Protection Officers.
6.3 If either Party receives any formal inquiry, complaint, claim or threat of action from a third
party (including, but not limited to, claims made by a supplier or requests for information made
under the Freedom of Information Act 2002) in relation to this MoU, the matter shall be
promptly referred to the Data Protection Officer of each Party (or its nominated
representatives). No action shall be taken in response to any such inquiry, complaint, claim
or action, to the extent that such response would adversely affect this MoU, without the prior
approval of the Data Controller (or its nominated representatives).

Nature and
purposes of the
processing
Personal data is collected via the specific Gov.UK citizens facing journey to
facilitate a number of processes set out within the law. These are
• Application and delivery of a voter authority certificate
• Application for an absent vote (postal or proxy vote)
• Application to register as an overseas elector
Application and production of a voter authority certificate
From May 2023 electors in the UK will be required to present photographic
identification to vote in polling stations in order to cast their ballots.
The purpose of the processing within the service is that electors who don’t not
possess one of the forms of identification on the approved list can apply via
new the Gov.uk service for a locally issued voter authority certificate to be able
to vote in person at a polling station.
Applications will capture personal data required for identity checks to be carried
out and for a Voter Authority Certificate. The voter authority certificate service
will make use of the automated DWP NINO identity check process. For the
identity of the elector the NINO provided will be checked against DWP
databases only, DLUHC and DWP will be independent controllers. Once these
checks are completed and all related personal data deleted, DLUHC will revert
to being a processor taking instruction from the ERO as per this agreement. In
some instances, where a NINO has not been provided, processing for the
purposes of the identity check could include the applicant uploading
documentary evidence from a list prescribed in law.
Application for an absent vote
The purpose of the processing is to enable electors to apply for apply for a
postal or proxy vote online for specified election or for a specified period. The
OAVA service will make use of the automated DWP NINO identity check
process. For the identity of the elector the NINO provided will be checked
against DWP databases only, DLUHC and DWP will be independent
controllers. Once these checks are completed and all related personal data
deleted, DLUHC will revert to being a processor taking instruction from the ERO
as per this agreement. In some instances, where a NINO has not provided,
processing will include the applicant uploading documentary evidence from a
list prescribed in law.
For the identity of the elector the NINO provided will be checked against DWP
databases only, DLUHC and DWP will be independent controllers. Once these

9
checks are completed and all related personal data deleted, DLUHC will revert
to being a processor taking instruction from the ERO as per this agreement. In
some instances, where a NINO has not been provided, processing for the
purposes of the identity check may include the applicant uploading
documentary evidence from a list prescribed in law.
Application to register as an overseas elector
The purpose of the processing is to enable those eligible electors who reside
abroad to apply to register to vote. The overseas electors register to vote
service will make use of the automated DWP NINO identity check process. For
the identity of the elector the NINO provided will be checked against DWP
databases only, DLUHC and DWP will be independent controllers. Once these
checks are completed and all related personal data deleted, DLUHC will revert
to being a processor taking instruction from the ERO as per this agreement. In
some instances, where a NINO has not provided, processing may include the
applicant uploading documentary evidence from a list prescribed in law.
In some instances where an applicant has been unable to evidence previous
electoral registration at the address specified, processing may include checks
of DWP records for previous residency at the specified address.
If overseas applicants were never registered in the UK before they left the
country, they will be applying in respect of their previous residence. In this
instance, they will be able to upload documents to demonstrate their connection
to their previous address.
If overseas applicants were under 18 when they left the UK, they may provide
evidence of their connection to a parent or guardian at the point of application,
such as a birth or adoption certificate.

Legal Basis for
Processing
The lawful basis for processing personal data is the below:
The lawful basis for the processing of data via the EROP - Article 6 (1) (e) -
processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public
interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller (“Public
Task”)

13
The public task is the new digital service which will support electors to vote by
helping them apply for an absent vote whilst proving their identity, and by
providing local authorities with the relevant information to process their vote.
Article 6(1)(c) provides a lawful basis for processing where: “processing is
necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is
subject.” (“Legal obligation”)
It will in fact be necessary to process this data to ensure that identity checking
takes place and that eligible electors can receive an absent vote, and thus cast
their vote in elections.
Voter authority certificate
The Voter Identification Regulations 2022 detail the requirements on electors
and the obligations on EROs with respect to the provision and the processing
of documentary evidence demonstrating a person’s identity for voting at a
polling station.
Online absent vote application services
EROs fulfil their legal duty to receive and issue postal and proxy votes under
The Representation of the People (England and Wales) Regulations 2001.
On receipt of an application under section 10ZC or 10ZD of the Representation
of the People Act 1983, a registration officer must disclose the name or names,
date of birth and national insurance number given under regulation 26 or 26A
to the Secretary of State.
Section 2 and Schedule 2 of the Elections Act 2022 enables the introduction of
regulations allowing application to be submitted via an online route and
provides for a new identity checking process to be applied to absent vote
applicants, which will apply to paper applications as well as to applications
made online.
The requirements of that processing of personal data are in The
Representation of the People (Postal and Proxy Voting Etc) (Amendment)
Regulations 2023 laid before Parliament on 3rd July 2023, due to be made by
31st October 2023, ahead of the service going live in October 2023.
Overseas electors
EROs fulfil their legal duty to maintain the electoral register which operates to
the franchise set in the law.
Section 14 1A of the Elections Act 2022 removes the 15 year limit for British
citizens living overseas. EROs are required to process personal data in this
way in to comply with The Representation of the People (Overseas Electors)
(Amendment) Regulations 2023.
