This Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on personal data sharing is not legally binding and no legal obligations or legal rights shall arise between the Parties from the provisions of this MoU (hereafter referred to as “this Agreement”).
By signing this MoU (i) the Parties do not intend to create contractual or other legal relations; and (ii) the Parties agree to adhere to the framework set out in the Agreement.
It has been agreed between DLUHC and Local Authorities to ensure agreement and appropriate arrangements are in place in respect of the sharing of Personal Data as Data Controllers.
The use of Personal Data governed by this data sharing Agreement (DSA) covers the processing of Personal Data provided by DLUHC, to be transferred to and by London Borough of Camden for the purpose set out in clause 4 below.
The transfer and use of Personal Data is necessary to deliver the Communities for Afghans project (CfA).
Given urgency for the sharing of Personal Data covered in this DSA, a Party accessing or otherwise using the data constitutes formal agreement to the terms of this DSA, in parallel to the execution clause 29 below.
DLUHC is an Independent Data Controller for the processing of the Personal Data up to the point that the Personal Data is provided to London Borough of Camden.
Citizens UK is the main Data Processor contracted by DLUHC for processing of its Personal Data.
It will directly receive Personal Data from London Borough of Camden and other parties delivering CfA.
For avoidance of doubt, as DLUHC’s Processor any direct sharing of Personal Data with Citizens UK is synonymous with sharing with DLUHC; DLUHC will be solely responsible for the purpose, use and security of the Personal Data from the point that Citizens UK receives it from London Borough of Camden.
London Borough of Camden is an Independent Data Controller from the point that Personal Data is provided to them by DLUHC to facilitate delivery of CfA, and/or it collects its own personal data for the purpose of delivering the project.
For avoidance of doubt, London Borough of Camden of the Personal Data from this point of receipt from DLUHC.
Similarly, where London Borough of Camden provides Personal Data to DLUHC; DLUHC will be solely responsible for the purpose, use and security of the Personal Data from this point of receipt from London Borough of Camden.
No Controller-Processor, or Joint Data Controller relationship will apply.

Section 10(5) and Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 6 of the DPA
The Parties agree that Personal Data used under this DSA shall be processed in accordance with the Data Protection Legislation.
Save to the extent that an exemption applies to limit or exclude any of them, compliance with the Data Protection Legislation shall include (but is not limited to) compliance with the Data Protection Principles as described in Article 5 of the UK GDPR.
Personal Data shall therefore be – 2.1. processed lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner (‘lawfulness, fairness and transparency’); 2.2. collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes (‘purpose limitation’); 2.3. adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimisation’); 2.4. accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date and that every reasonable step is taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate, having regard to the purposes for which they are processed, are erased or rectified without delay (‘accuracy’); 2.5. kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed (‘storage limitation’); 2.6. processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organisational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).
Processed in accordance with the UK GDPR and demonstrating our compliance.
The parties undertake to have in place throughout the Term appropriate technical and organisational security measures to:
1.1. prevent: 1.1.1. unauthorised or unlawful processing of the Personal Data; and 1.1.2. the accidental loss or destruction of, or damage to, the Personal Data; and 1.2. ensure a level of security appropriate to: 1.2.1. the harm that might result from such unauthorised or unlawful processing or accidental loss, destruction or damage; and 1.2.2. the nature of the Personal Data to be protected.
The Parties shall keep security measures under review and shall carry out such updates as are appropriate throughout the Term.
It is the responsibility of each party to ensure that its staff members are appropriately trained to handle and process the Project Personal Data in accordance with the technical and organisational security measures set out in Annex 1 together with any other applicable national data protection laws and guidance and have entered into confidentiality agreements relating to the processing of personal data.
The level, content and regularity of training referred to in clause 2 shall be proportionate to the staff members' role, responsibility and frequency with respect to their handling and processing of the Project Personal Data.
The Parties shall each comply with its obligation to report a Personal Data Breach to the appropriate Supervisory Authority and (where applicable) data subjects under Article 33 of the UK GDPR and shall each inform the other party within 24 hours of becoming aware of any Personal Data Breach irrespective of whether there is a requirement to notify any Supervisory Authority or data subject(s).
The Parties agree to provide reasonable assistance as is necessary to each other to facilitate the handling of any Personal Data Breach in an expeditious and compliant manner.
The Parties will comply in full with the rights of Data Subjects in Chapter III of the UK GDPR where applicable.
Save where they are limited or excluded by an exemption these may include the right to – 2.1. Object – the right of the Data Subject to request that their Personal Data is not processed for certain purposes.
Erasure - the right of the Data Subject to request that their Personal Data is erased where one of the statutory grounds applies.
Data portability – the right of the Data Subject to obtain Personal Data and reuse their Personal Data for their own purposes across different services in certain circumstances.
Restrict processing - the right of the Data Subject to request that the processing of their Personal Data is restricted in certain circumstances – for example, where accuracy is contested.
Rectification - the right of the Data Subject to request that any inaccuracies in their Personal Data are rectified without delay.
Request that any incomplete personal data is completed, including by means of a supplementary statement.
Access - the right of the Data Subject to request access to information about how their Personal Data is processed and to request a copy of that personal data.
Each Party shall notify the other(s) as soon as possible or within 24 hours (whichever is sooner) of becoming aware of a Data Protection Breach in respect of Personal Data used under this Agreement.
The Parties agree that where appropriate, they will work together and ensure co-operation regarding the handling of such a breach.
The Parties will ensure that they take all reasonable steps to ensure the reliability and integrity of its Personnel who have access to the Personal Data which is processed under this DSA that they are aware of their obligations under the Data Protection Legislation and in particular that they have had appropriate training on the handling of Personal Data.
The Parties are aware of the prohibition in Article 44 of the UK GDPR against the transfer of Personal Data used under this DSA to ‘third countries’ (as described in that Article) unless certain conditions are met.
In the event that either is in any doubt about the legality of an international transfer of the personal data processed under this agreement, it shall consult the other as appropriate.
The Parties shall notify the other without delay in the event that, in relation to the personal data processed under this Agreement, it – 1.1. receives any communication from the Information Commissioner or any other regulatory authority; 1.2. receives a letter before action or other communication indicating an intention to commence legal proceedings in respect of Personal Data processed under this DSA;
1.3. receives a complaint, notice, communication or penalty which and agree to collaborate on a response to such an enquiry.
The Parties shall work together regarding the response to any complaint, communication or request from the Information Commissioner or other regulatory authority or any threatened litigation by a third party in relation to the Personal Data processed under this Agreement.
The parties shall transfer necessary Personal Data requested under this Agreement, regardless of its format, only via secure means of transit.
English local authorities will share data with DLUHC via the DELTA system.
Both Parties will ensure that any further Data Processor (or subprocessor) engaged by either one of them, shall only receive personal data via secure means of transit, regardless of its format.
The Parties’ Privacy Notices shall reflect the data processing activities and any changes made to these as established under this Agreement, subject to the restriction stated in clause 4.2 above.
The Parties shall maintain complete and accurate processing of records to demonstrate their compliance with the Data Protection Legislation in relation to the Personal Data processed under this Agreement.
In accordance with the principle of data minimisation the Parties shall delete their relevant personal data as required by the records retention schedules and within 2 years of the closure of the scheme unless DLUHC identifies that its continued retention is unnecessary before that point.
The Parties shall not retain or process Personal Data for longer than is necessary to carry out the Agreed Purposes.
This includes any statutory, legal or professional retention periods applicable to the personal data.
This Agreement shall commence on 01 July 2024 and will run for 6 months from this date unless terminated earlier in accordance with clause 23.2.
This Agreement can be terminated before its end date as per clause 23.1 only via mutual agreement between both Parties, with at least three months' notice in writing, unless both Parties are agreed that a reduced notice period is acceptable.
If either of the Parties has any issues, concerns or complaints about this Agreement it shall notify the other and they shall, acting in good faith, seek to resolve the issue by negotiations between themselves, and follow the resolution process.
In the event of a dispute or claim brought by a data subject or the Supervisory Authority concerning the processing of Personal Data against either or both Parties, the Parties will inform each other about any such disputes or claims and will co-operate with a view to settling them amicably in a timely fashion.
The Parties agree to respond to any generally available nonbinding mediation procedure initiated by a data subject or by the Supervisory Authority.
If they do participate in the proceedings, the parties may elect to do so remotely (such as by telephone or other electronic means).
The Parties also agree to consider participating in any other arbitration, mediation or other dispute resolution proceedings developed for data protection disputes.
Each Party shall abide by a decision of a competent court in England and Wales or of that Supervisory Authority.
The Parties shall each appoint a nominated representative to whom any disputes arising from the operation of this Agreement shall be referred.
If the Parties are unable to agree a matter arising under the terms and clauses of this Agreement, such a dispute shall be referred to the senior officers of the nominated representatives for resolution.
The decision of the senior officers shall bind all Parties and be treated as resolving the dispute.
The Parties shall, where a dispute cannot be resolved by agreement between their senior officers, appoint an adjudicator whose decision shall be final.
In the event of a dispute or claim brought by a data subject or the Information Commissioner’s Office concerning the processing of shared Personal Data against either of the Parties, the Parties will inform each other about any such disputes or claims and will cooperate with a view to settling them amicably in a timely fashion.
This Agreement may only be varied by written agreement between the Parties.
