Background The London Innovation and Improvement Alliance (LIIA) is a standing partnership of London Local Authorities, working together for the benefit of vulnerable children in the capital.
Part of the LIIA project is a pan-London Sector-Led Improvement analysis team, hosted at Waltham Forest Borough Council, who produce pan-London analyses for the London Directors of Children's Services.
A successful pilot phase ran in Jan-March 2021 with Enfield, Islington, Merton, Wandsworth, Richmond, and Kingston to create a process to safely, legally, and ethically take in individual-level data where appropriate, and create analyses based on it.
Building on the learning from the pilot phase, this Agreement provides the basis to expand the project to cover all 32 London Boroughs and the City of London Corporation.
This will create an infrastructure that has the legal and ethical framework to enable child-level data sharing and a secure & efficient data network that makes this practical and accessible for each LA in the LIIA without increasing burdens or cost unnecessarily or disproportionally.
The infrastructure is designed to provide impact at scale, while maximising process efficiency and data security.
It represents a key outcome of the Pan London Data Accelerator Programme.
Data specialists Social Finance are advising, with a focus on ensuring that the process embeds the principles of privacy by design, that the processing remains proportionate to the benefits, and that the process can be set up with the minimum level of additional work for LA teams.
Expanding the project to the whole of London requires a robust and secure data hosting infrastructure to facilitate the uploading, processing, and analysing of child-level data from multiple local authorities.
The London Datastore, a data-sharing platform created by the London Greater Authority, provides such an infrastructure and will be the key partner for hosting and processing LA data.
The overall framework will have LAs deposit data on the London Datastore, where it will be processed to create pan-London datasets, which will also be stored on the London Datastore.
This Agreement will document and authorise this infrastructure.
Access to pan-London datasets will be granted on a case-by-case basis to named individuals at 'analytical partners' (sub-processors as described by Clause 10.1) to conduct further data transformation and dissemination of analytical outputs.
Authorisations for these analytical partners will be managed as additional Schedules to this Agreement.
This technical and legal framework has the endorsement of the LIIA board and Information Governance for London.
The LIIA Child Level Data Project has now launched, with all 33 London Boroughs signed up to share data and data from the SSDA903 shared by all Boroughs for one of the three starting use cases, PanLondon Sufficiency Analysis.
Reporting from this use case has also gone live and has being shared with approx.
Changes to the agreement are being proposed that will make amendments to the core data flows and to existing use cases, and will also add new use cases to the platform, following the approval of the LIIA Board.
In line with the process established with Information Governance for London, these amendments will be scrutinised by one or more members of Information Governance for London, before being reviewed by the group in two monthly meetings.
Only following endorsement by the body will each LA be asked to approve these changes through Information Sharing Gateway, making them live changes to the agreement.
One of the changes is to the existing data flow structure, with the data ingress, processing and egress role previously played by GLA (the London DataStore) now taken on by Social Finance, who are building a novel data platform for this purpose.
The reason for this change is that the London Data Store, while robust at dealing with open access data, lacks some of the primary security features necessary for handling data containing personally identifiable information.
The full details of this will be described in an amended Schedule 2 of this agreement.
As this is a significant change to the existing agreement, two members of Information Governance for London are taking an active role in the scoping and development of this platform to ensure that it meets the data security standards necessary for the data being shared.

Schedule 2 (Data hosting, flows and security) Context The London Data Platform is a proposed data-sharing platform created by a partnership between LIIA, the Greater London Authority, and Social Finance, a not-for-profit consultancy with substantial technical and information governance expertise.
It is required because local public services, most notably local authority children’s services, are increasingly finding it necessary to share personal sensitive data with one another and with suppliers in order to deliver on their statutory responsibilities.
Hosting data within the London Data Platform and running analysis within its secure environment will allow for increased data security, as it drastically reduces the risk of data breaches associated with email transfers, data downloads or holding data locally.
The architecture of the platform has been designed specifically with this use-case in mind and it holds data security as its core principle.
This architecture is detailed in “Data security” below.
Social Finance will act as architectural principal for this project and will thus be responsible for the role of primary security contact and specifier.
Amazon Web Services will be the service provider.
Details of the appropriate certifications are outlined in the sections below.
Data flows The data shared by the CONTROLLER goes through the following steps: Upload data Prepare data for analysis Analyse data Share analysis These are expanded upon below and are represented in the data flow diagram (Figure 1).
The flows in steps 1 and 2 above are the same for all data processing activities covered by this agreement.
Therefore, they are described in this schedule only.
The flows in steps 3 and 4 are specific to each processing activity (e.g. Pan-London Sufficiency Analysis involves different flows to Social Worker Workforce Analysis as they involve different organisations processing data).
Therefore: Steps 3 and 4 are described in detail in the schedules created for each of those activities.
The only material we provide here regarding steps 3 and 4 is a generic description (e.g. PROCESSOR does X rather than named organisation does X).
Upload data The CONTROLLER is given access to a private folder in the London Data Platform and uploads the data there.
This folder is only accessible by authorised personnel from the CONTROLLER and the London Data Platform.
Preparing the data of a single CONTROLLER The PROCESSOR runs scripts within the London Data Platform environment to: Degrade certain data fields to protect data subject anonymity, e.g. date of birth transformed into month of birth; postcode transformed into postcode sector
Assess data quality (missing values, logically inconsistent values) At the end of this process, the data is pseudonymised.
Identification of individuals would only be possible with access to the CONTROLLER’s case management system.
The CONTROLLER’s processed dataset is deposited back in the CONTROLLER’s private folder; the CONTROLLER can download the processed dataset for analysis.
Merging all Borough’s data together An additional process within the London Data Platform environment merges the datasets from all CONTROLLERS into a single pan-London dataset.
During this process, additional data minimisation and data degradation steps may occur to ensure that only the data required for pan-London analysis is accessible.
These additional steps will be detailed in Schedules 3 onwards.
The processed dataset is deposited into the PROCESSOR’s private folder (“LIIA Analysis folder”) in the London Data Platform.
Analyse data Typically, each specific analysis (e.g. Pan-London Sufficiency Analysis) requires only a subset of the full dataset.
It may require: Fewer than the full set of data subjects (e.g. only children in care, rather than all children with a social worker) Fewer than the full set of fields describing each data subject (e.g. only information about the children’s demographics and the placement, rather than information about needs and disabilities) Data from a shorter period of time than is available in the full dataset (e.g. just the last 12m) Therefore, in line with the principle of privacy by design a specific dataset is created for each analysis which contains the minimum data required for that analysis.
These are stored in the LIIA Analysis folder.
Access to each of these datasets in the LIIA Analysis folder is restricted to named individuals at the PROCESSOR (or named sub-processor) with direct involvement in analyses of that dataset, as described in Schedules 3 onwards.
The PROCESSOR accesses the dataset in the LIIA Analysis folder in one of two ways: Local download via a secure username/password combination.
The dataset is then stored in a restricted area of the PROCESSOR’s data warehouse with access granted only to staff directly involved in its analysis.
Remote access via a secure link, connecting directly from within analytical software (e.g. Power BI).
The dataset is stored in the cache of the analytical software only, with access granted only to staff directly involved in its analysis.
Analysis conducted on the processed dataset is described in the relevant Schedule below.
Share the analysis Aggregated analytical outputs are shared with all Boroughs via securely hosted software (e.g. Power BI).
In all shared outputs, it is no longer possible to identify individual data points.
Boroughs will be identifiable in these shared analyses, allowing benchmarking and comparison between boroughs.
Where appropriate the LIIA d&i team will provide pan-London aggregate anonymous data for work commissioned or approved by ALDCS/LIIA or as agreed with partner organisations (where this data has already been collected through the LIIA CLD project) and so help reduce burdens on individual LA data & performance teams (relates to all use cases).

Data retention Unless otherwise specified, data will be retained until six years have elapsed since data collection.
Previous analysis finds that six years is the minimum for good quality journey analysis (an analytical approach we expect to employ), but that having more than six years’ data does not materially help to answer new questions or answer existing questions with greater certainty or granularity.
Maintenance of this retention is implemented as part of the automated processing of data on the London Data Platform.
On termination of this agreement, all data will be deleted from the CONTROLLER’s private folder and the LIIA Analysis folder.
The CONTROLLER can request the deletion of data at any time before the termination date.
London Data Platform infrastructure The application is architected to be secure by design.
It follows National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) best practice for cloud applications and its use of Amazon Web Services for all core application components means that it is based on the most solid and robust of cloud platforms.
Specifically, it adheres to the NCSC guiding principles as follows: Principle 1 - Data in transit protection The Data Platform will use the latest SSL certificates.
Connections will be 128bit encrypted and authenticated using TLS 1.2. (data encrypted in transit) Principle 2 - Asset protection and resilience Data will be hosted on Amazon Web Services (AWS) in a UK availability zone and a UK data centre.
The AWS data centre is secure and highly monitored (full list of procedures in place available here).
Data tables will be stored in AWS S3 buckets protected by a private key/user password combination.
(data encrypted at rest) AWS is certified ISO 27001 (full list of AWS certifications available here).
Principle 3 - Separation between customers Robust use of Identity and Access Management (IAM) prevents users accessing or processing data or systems outside their own.
Principle 4 - Operational security The platform will be subject to robust and well-defined operational processes Releases will be timely and well managed through a deployment management framework Incident management will have clear procedures and communication will be timely Principle 5 – Governance framework A governance framework will be established to clearly define feature extension signoff, administration oversight and eventual handoff of the system to a suitable long term owner as required.
Principle 6 - Personnel security AWS personnel are subject to robust vetting and security checks.
Access to systems from inside the London Data Platform will be limited to those identified in the data flows section above.
Principle 7 –Secure development Engineering will follow software development best practices, including merge protections and code review Deployment will be automated and follow a test > release process across multiple environments before production Code will be subjected to multiple levels of automated and manual testing Principle 8 - Supply chain security Software dependencies will be tracked and vulnerabilities automatically reported Dependencies in code run on the processing environment will be subject to a robust and well managed allow-list Principles 9 and 10 - Secure user management/ Identity and authentication Uploads will be Private as default.
Single-sign-on via major identity service providers (Microsoft Active Directory, Google Cloud Identity) will provide best in class password complexity enforcement and multi-factorauthentication requirements.
A full audit log for user access and data movement will be recorded Principle 11 - External interface protection Exposed user interfaces will follow best practice for user facing application security – including cross-site scripting protection and cross-site request forgery protection
The minimum required API will be exposed to reduce the available attack surface to the smallest possible Principle 12 – Secure Services Administration Administration of AWS services and infrastructure is carried out entirely through the AWS console and administration system which is subject to the resiliency and security principles laid down by Amazon themselves.
Principle13 - Resilience and auditing The application will be fully penetration tested before production release is granted.
Regular security audits by qualified third-party organisations will be carried out.
Data protection from loss and lack of availability on AWS is covered by their business continuity and disaster recovery policy.
Principle 14 – Security by Design and Default The design of the system is such that users are subject to guardrails which prevent accidental misuse.
Code run on the platform will be sandboxed and airgapped from the wider internet Controls and role based access will prevent data leaking between users or clients
Schedule 6 (Sharing SEND insights; SEN2 Special educational needs analysis – part of Sharing Children’s Services data insights) Nature and purpose of analysis This schedule provides details of the data flow for Special educational needs data through the LIIA platform.
The data submitted by the CONTROLLER is one standard return created by all Children’s Services departments: Special Educational Needs return (SEN2) The process will enable pan-London analysis to understand how the services provided and the outcomes experienced by children and young people compare across London Boroughs.
The purpose of the analysis is to accelerate service improvement and monitor equalities as required under section 17 of the Children Act 1989 “to safeguard and promote the welfare of children in need in their area”, section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 to deliver the “public sector equality duty” and various sections of the SEN Code of Conduct.
Accelerating service improvement – by enabling the identification and prioritisation of opportunities for improvement, and the identification of good practice in other boroughs; b.
Monitoring equalities – by enabling comparative analysis of the prevalence of key SEND interventions being used with families of different ethnicities; Through this, the project aims to benefit vulnerable children, young people, and their families by improving the quality of services which provide necessary support for their special educational needs, reducing systemic discrimination (should there be evidence of this being present in London) and to help them to develop to their full potential.
Data to be shared The data submitted by the CONTROLLER will include personal data, including special category data, about: children and young people for whom a Local Authority has carried our actions prescribed in the SEN Code of Conduct: o an initial request for an Education, Health and Care (EHC) assessment o an EHC assessment o maintained an EHC plan o mediation as set out in the SEN Code of Conduct o a tribunal as set out in the SEN Code of Conduct o Special Educational Need (SEN) services Personal data, including special category data to be processed: Surname, forename, unique identifiers, including unique pupil number, unique learner number and LA-issued identifiers demographic information, including gender, date of birth and ethnicity geographic identifiers, including home postcode, Establishment unique reference number and/or UK Provider reference number SEN information, including dates, codes for SEN needs, and outcomes, for: o EHC requests and assessments o mediations o tribunals
