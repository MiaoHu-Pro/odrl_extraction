The Secretary of State delegates a number of his/her public health functions to NHS England.
The public health functions agreement 2017 – 2018 enables NHS England to commission certain public health services which will drive improvements in population health, and this agreement sets out the service specifications which are to be commissioned in order to satisfy those public health functions.
One such service is the CHIS, and so it is commissioned by NHS England in order to discharge part of their public health duties.
The CHIS initiative follows a tender process by NHS England (London) which resulted in the appointment of three service providers (referenced as “CHIS Hub Service Providers”) on a 5-year (plus 2 year) contract from 1st April 2017 to serve London subject to commissioner contract extension.
A single London-wide CHIS database has replaced the 27 former CHIS provider databases.
The following CHIS Hub Service Providers cover the capital: InHealth Intelligence Ltd (IHI) delivers the North West London CHIS Hub service and the South East London CHIS Hub service North East London NHS Foundation Trust (NELFT) delivers the North East London CHIS Hub service, and Your Healthcare CIC (Your Healthcare) delivers the South West London CHIS Hub service.

Once in agreed form this Agreement will support the Service to meet its strategic objectives namely to “Know where every child is” and “How well they are” through the modernisation of Child Health Information Systems and their new focus on collecting, collating, analysing and reporting.

Legal Basis for Processing and Sharing The legal basis – the processing and sharing of data with the Service is necessary for medical purposes and is being initiated by your Organisation – the “Participant”.
This is in the context of the CHIS Hub Service Providers being contracted by NHS England, under Section 7A of the National Health Service Act 2006.
This agreement focuses on achieving positive health outcomes for the population and reducing health inequalities through the provision of the CHIS.
The CHIS Hub Service Providers will ensure they only processes data as reflected by this and
similar Agreements, and in accordance with the Data Protection Act 2018 ("DPA 2018") which supplements the General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR").
Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently.
There must be a lawful basis for processing under Article 6 of GDPR.
In addition, as this processing involves special category personal data (i.e. health data), then there must also be a condition for processing under Article 9 of GDPR.
Organisations (who are the Data Controllers) can rely on the following Articles (under GDPR) for the provision of sharing of data with the CHIS service: Article 6(1)(c) -processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject and/or Article 6(1)(e) -processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller.
Moving onto the additional basis for processing required by special categories of personal data (please note no special categories data is required to be provided from borough councils), provision of care and treatment can rely upon: Article 9(2)(h) -processing is necessary for the purposes of preventive or occupational medicine, for the assessment of the working capacity of the employee, medical diagnosis, the provision of health or social care or treatment or the management of health or social care systems and services on the basis of Union or Member State law or pursuant to contract with a health professional.
The sharing of patient identifiable data (PID) will only take place with the Data Controllers who participate in the Service, however other organisations are able to access patient level, but truly anonymised data and Aggregate Only data (as detailed in SCHEDULE 2).
Under the DPA 2018 / GDPR, Data Controllers are expected to be much more transparent with patients about how their data will be processed.
For the reasons outlined above, you will not need patient consent to share and access information through the CHIS.
However, your Privacy Notice should explain that data will be shared with the CHIS.
In addition to the requirements of the data protection legislation, any sharing of personal data must also take place in accordance with the common law duty of confidentiality.
The duty does not require consent from a patient where the proposed use of their data is either for individual care or in the public interest (which it is here as it directly benefits patient and public health).
Good practice suggests that although consent is not required, patients should be told how their data will be used and this should be set out in your Privacy Notice (as referenced in the previous paragraph).
This is a matter for all Data Controllers to address themselves.
These flows of patient data from your Organisation to the CHIS Hub Service Providers have a legal basis and this represents “business as usual”.
The processing and sharing of data with other NHS Organisations is for medical purposes and is being initiated by your Organisation and is in alignment with the legal obligations to share patient information for the purposes of direct patient care, pursuant to section 251B of the Health and Social Care Act 2012.
The sharing of data in support of care is supported by the Children Act 1989.
The flow of patient identifiable data concerning children aged 0-19 inclusive between the Participant and the CHIS occurs presently and is an established information flow in support of the Healthy Child Programme.
These flows of patient data from organisations involved in the care of children to the London CHIS Hub Service Providers have a legal basis and this represents “business as usual”.
Some aggregate only data (numbers and percentages only) and anonymised data will be shared with NHS England as part of their management of the service and for invoice validation.
Whilst the DPA 2018/GDPR and Health & Social Care Act 2006 are concerned with the appropriateness of sharing patient identifiable data, anonymised or aggregate data are not subject to these Acts, provided that the patients cannot be identified from the data.
How the data export works There are a range of solutions being delivered by the Service; the existing e-mail-based communication methods with CSV file-based data sharing arrangements will over time be replaced by modern and secure messaging e.g. HL7 and FHIR or using secure FTP.
Investments are being made to modernise the information flows in support of this data sharing.
As part of the implementation of each new method of data provision a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) will be produced and risks assessed and mitigated.
