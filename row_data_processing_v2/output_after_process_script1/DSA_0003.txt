Restart is a multi-partner project set up with the aims to improve responses to perpetrators of domestic abuse in families that are being supported by Children’s Social Care via a coordinated multi-agency response.
By holding the abusive parent to account for their behaviour, and responsible for making change, this project seeks to: Identify and respond to patterns of domestic abuse at an earlier stage for families, improving safety and long-term outcomes for child and adult victims Build motivation and facilitate access to behaviour change interventions for the abusive parent Where required, facilitate access to alternative accommodation for the abusive parent ensuring that child and adult victim-survivors remain safe at home with space for action Reduce repeat and serial incidents of domestic abuse in the same, or in future, relationships Prevent escalation and the risk of adult and child victim-survivors being the ones needing to move/flee to safer accommodation Provide training and capacity building for CSC practitioners to enable CSC to effectively hold abusive parents to account Shift thinking and approaches in relation to the prevention of family homelessness by working with Housing teams on accommodation pathways SafeLives is acting as Lead partner on Restart, which has been successful in securing grant funding from the Home Office with match funding from MOPAC in order to fund the activities and work of all partners involved in the project.
The other Restart partners, which have been commissioned to deliver this pilot are: Respect, Cranstoun and Standing Together Against Domestic Abuse (presenting Domestic Abuse Housing Alliance).
They will be providing services across the London boroughs of Camden, Croydon, Sutton, Havering and Westminster, in order to achieve the aims of the project.

DATA SUBJECTS' RIGHTS 1 Data Subjects have the right to obtain certain information about the processing of their Personal Data through a Subject Access Request.
Data Subjects may also request rectification, erasure or blocking of their Personal Data.
The Parties shall maintain a record of Subject Access Requests, the decisions made and any information that was exchanged.
Records must include copies of the request for information, details of the data accessed and shared and where relevant, notes of any meeting, correspondence or phone calls relating to the request, together with relevant dates.
The Parties agree that the responsibility for complying with a Subject Access Request falls to Party receiving the Subject Access Request in respect of the Personal Data held by that Party.
The Parties agree to provide reasonable and prompt assistance (within 5 business days of such a request for assistance) at their own expense as is necessary to each other to enable them to comply with Subject Access Requests and to respond to any other queries or complaints from Data Subjects.

SECURITY AND TRAINING 1 The Parties agree to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect the Personal Data in their possession against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, damage, alteration or disclosure, including but not limited to: a) ensuring IT equipment, including portable equipment is kept in lockable areas when unattended; b) not leaving portable equipment containing the Personal Data unattended; c) ensuring Staff use appropriate secure passwords for logging into systems or databases containing the Personal Data; d) ensuring that all IT equipment is protected by antivirus software, firewalls, passwords and suitable encryption devices; e) ensuring that any Special Category Personal Data is stored and transferred (including where stored or transferred on portable devices or removable media) using industry standard 256-bit AES encryption or suitable equivalent; f) limiting access to relevant databases and systems to those Staff who need to have access to the Personal Data, and ensuring that passwords are changed and updated regularly to prevent inappropriate access when individuals are no longer engaged by the Party; g) conducting regular threat assessment or penetration testing on systems; h) ensuring all Staff handling Personal Data have been made aware of their responsibilities with regards to handling of the Personal Data; and i) allowing for inspections and assessments to be undertaken by the other Party in respect of the security measures taken, or producing evidence of those measures if requested.
PERSONAL DATA BREACHES AND REPORTING PROCEDURES 1 The Parties shall ensure they do not knowingly or negligently do or omit to do anything which places either Party in breach of the Data Protection Legislation.
Each Party shall inform the other immediately if it: a) is in breach of the Protective Measures requirements; b) receives a request to rectify, block or erase the Personal Data; c) receives any complaint or communication relating to either Party's obligations under the Data Protection Legislation; d) receives any communication from the Information Commissioner or any other regulatory authority in connection with the Personal Data; e) receives a request from any third party for disclosure of the Personal Data where compliance with such request is required or purported to be required by Law; or f) becomes aware of a Data Loss Event, and without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours, shall notify the other Parties, by: (i) contacting the Data Protection Officer(s) as detailed within the Schedule; and (ii) informing them of the Data Loss Event, including in its notification: (A) a description of the Data Loss Event including where possible, the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects concerned and the categories and approximate number of data records concerned; and (B) a description of the measures taken or proposes to take to address the Data Loss Event, including, where appropriate, measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.
Following the notification of a Data Loss Event, the informing Party shall immediately assess the risks and consequences of the Data Loss Event and take measures, including: (i) to mitigate the possible adverse effects of the Data Loss Event; and (ii) to protect the data and the interests/safety of the Data Subject and/or Data Controller, and inform the other Parties as to the measures that it has taken.
The informing Party’s obligation to notify shall include the provision of further information in phases, as details become available.

The social care practitioner will gain consent from the potential service user and their partner and obtain signed confidentiality and information sharing agreements from both parties.
Behaviour change intervention and risk management: Once consent is obtained, the early intervention practitioner will work closely with the individual on 1:1 behaviour change work.
Information relevant to assessing risk and behaviour will be recorded and shared with the social care practitioner.
The expectation is that the Early Intervention Practitioner and Social Care Practitioner will convene a professionals’ meeting a minimum of once per week to discuss updates, share information pertinent to risk, progress and intervention planning, including planning around the safety and wellbeing of the partner and family.
Information will be shared via protected electronic files, secure email or telephone and face-to-face meeting.
Data Storage: Personal data captured for the purposes of case assessment, planning and risk management will be held on Cranstoun’s secure case management system (Iizuka), and will be shared only with the Early Intervention Practitioners and Restart project partners and staff member for the purposes of audit, quality assurance and case supervision.
Onward referrals: Information sharing regarding individuals and families referred into the service will be based on consent, and will occur within local statutory MARAC and MASH information sharing and data protection frameworks.
If during the intervention (or at any time thereafter) a victim is identified as being at high risk of harm, a referral should be made to the high risk IDVA team as soon as possible, and to MARAC if the client reaches the MARAC threshold.
Evaluation: Anonymised data will be shared with the evaluator (Social Care Institute for Excellence) for the purposes of learning and evaluation.
