There are various acts of parliament which provide the legal power to share information in the right circumstance.
This protocol formalises how information will be shared between the various partners of the Rescue and Response Project and all London Boroughs.
The project covers any young people who live in London and have been identified as being exploited through County Lines.
This document is not a legally binding document.
It aims to provide the basis for an agreement between the agencies and other organisations engaged in the Rescue and Response project to facilitate and govern the efficient, effective and secure sharing of good quality information.
It sets out: The principles underpinning information sharing The general purposes for information sharing The responsibilities and commitments of partners to this agreement The arrangements for monitoring and review This protocol aligns with all other protocols to which agencies may already be signatories and does not in any way supersede those existing agreements.
It is not intended that this document be definitive or exhaustive, it is recognised that as policy develops and implementation arrangements mature, this protocol will need to be reviewed and amended in light of new information sharing requirements to ensure that it is ‘fit for purpose’.
As this protocol aims to provide a set of guiding principles for information sharing within the context of the Rescue and Response project to which partners can sign up, its purpose is not to be specific.
It is a key principle of this agreement that this protocol should be underpinned by a series of specific data sharing agreements that detail the specific data/information sharing requirements between agencies.
Policy context The Rescue and Response project will develop and deliver a support service for young people who are vulnerable and caught up in county lines drug distribution networks across London.
This issue brings together gangs, national policing strategy, CSE, trafficking, exploitation and vulnerability; underpinned by a complex range of social and structural disadvantage.
The need has been identified but is so far only partly understood and sporadically mapped, with support models untested at any scale.
This project will be the first county lines support service that operates at scale - pan London and for three years.
The project includes both support providers and intelligence analysis working together.
It is led by a coalition of London boroughs (Brent, Islington, Tower Hamlets and Lewisham) with support from Metropolitan
In order for the development of the Rescue and Response project to be successful, it is essential that all agencies and organisations engaged in its development and implementation are empowered and committed to share good quality and relevant information in a responsible and secure way.
This agreement deals with the need to share personal information in order to identify young people from London who are being exploited through County Lines, to then monitor them, support them by referring to voluntary agencies, and possibly conduct enforcement action against those linked to County Lines.
This work will take place as part of the Rescue and Response project.
It seeks to fulfil the aims and ambitions outlined in the MOPAC Police and Crime Plan for multiple agencies to: work together to keep children and young people safe, prevent violence against women and girls, reduce reoffending and repeat victimisation as well as enabling the Community Safety Partnership to meet its Statutory Obligations.
It is intended to focus partnership activity on identifying and supporting young people from London who are being exploited through county lines, as well as targeting those offenders who are linked to county lines exploitation.
Information will be shared in order to make informed decisions about the young people referred to the Rescue and Response project and which agency will provide direct support to those young people.
Partner agencies will pool their information together regarding the young people identified as being exploited.
The list of young people identified and referred will be: shared with the London borough they reside in, or come from; identified for support either through the Rescue and Response project, or alternative borough based interventions; an assessment made as to their level of engagement with partners and the most appropriate agency provide support intervention.
This exchange of information will continue as the young people are supported by the partners of this multi-agency scheme.
This will be an ongoing process as young people’s risk increases, move geographically, and where relevant, come out of prison.
This will mean that there will always be young people who are being considered for the project, move within the project and face removal from the project.

The aims and objectives of the Rescue and Response Project is to provide effective support to young people from London being exploited through County Lines programme, and identify young people earlier, in order to:  Reduce crime  Safeguard young people earlier  Reduction in recorded criminal behaviour of the young people referred  Increase in number of Young People identified as being exploited  Increased engagement with services  Better understanding of the problem and networks  Increase in number of Young people identified as being at risk of county lines exploitation  Reduce reoffending,  Make safer communities,  Improve public confidence in the criminal justice system, The information sharing under this agreement will allow partners to provide the best range of services to the young people whilst addressing any continuing offending or safeguarding concerns.

In signing up to this Protocol the signatories agree and commit to the following principles: This protocol aims to align with individual partner agency statutory, legal and common law duties.
This agreement is to be entered into alongside any existing protocols, procedures, policies and guidance to which the partner already adheres and does not supersede them.
This protocol will be underpinned by individual data sharing agreements that will govern data sharing transactions between partners.
Information will only be used for the purposes stated in this protocol, and as detailed in individual information sharing agreements.
Partner agencies comply with the requirements of the Data Protection Act 2018 and in particular the data protection principles.
Partner agencies support, endorse and promote the accurate, timely, secure and confidential sharing of information for the purposes stated in this protocol.
Where it is agreed that it is necessary to share personal information, it will be shared only on a ‘need to know’ basis and this will be detailed in the individual information sharing agreements.
All other information will be statistical and aggregated.
Personal and sensitive information will only be shared under this agreement where there is a statutory power to do so and the conditions for processing as determined in the Data Protection Act 2018 can be met.
Agencies agree to ensure that data sharing takes place in accordance with their legal, statutory and common law duties and that responsibility for ensuring that they have adequate notifications, privacy notices, retention schedules, policies, procedures and guidance to do so remains with them.
All information shared shall be classified according to the Government Security Classifications Policy (GSCP).
The handling and access of the data shall comply with the requirements of this classification.
In the event of a Security Incident, the responsible Partner Organisation should immediately inform the Governing Group and all other affected Partner Organisations (usually the disclosing Partner Organisation(s)) with as many details as known at that time and regularly update the relevant Partner Organisations and Governing Group thereafter, including any subsequent investigation report or remedial actions.
Partner Organisations will follow the decision tree at Annex C.
Information shared pursuant to this Protocol may not be shared with any other organisation not a signatory to this Protocol without prior consent of the relevant Partner Organisation and/or data subject.
Each Partner Organisation shall comply with individual rights as defined under the Data Protection Act 2018, Freedom of Information Act 2005 and the Human Rights Act 1998.
All information will be supplied in line with the relevant standards for information quality and security.
The systems and technical measures used by each Partner Organisation for the sharing of personal information shall be specified in any information sharing agreement.
Each Partner Organisation shall minimise the use of paper in sharing and storing information.
Information in transit shall be secure and encrypted.
Use of removable storage devices shall be prohibited.
Paper in transit shall only be undertaken if adequate safeguard are in place and the information is minimised.
Use of public WI-FI shall be prohibited.
If a Partner Organisation is aware of a material inaccuracy or omission in information that it shares under an Information Sharing Agreement, the Partner Organisation must inform the recipient of that inaccuracy or omission.
Each Partner Organisation will nominate a person as a key contact to deal with queries and requests for information under this Protocol.
Each Partner Organisation is responsible for putting into place effective procedures to address complaints about data sharing and subject access requests relating directly to this Protocol.
Information about these procedures should be made available to data subjects.
Any complaints about data sharing relating directly to this Protocol should be directed promptly to the Data Protection Officer or Information Governance Manager of the relevant Partner Organisation.
