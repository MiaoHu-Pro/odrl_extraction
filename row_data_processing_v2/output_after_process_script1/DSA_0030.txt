PURPOSE 1 This Agreement sets out the framework for the sharing of Personal Data between the Parties as Data Controllers and defines the principles and procedures that the Parties shall adhere to and the responsibilities the Parties owe to each other.
The Personal Data subject to this Agreement is listed within the Schedule.
The sharing of Personal Data is necessary to support the Agreed Purposes as listed within the Schedule.
It is recognised that there are significant benefits to the Parties of having access to the Shared Personal Data to achieve the Agreed Purposes.
This Agreement is required to ensure that where Personal Data may be accessed, such access will at all times comply with the Data Protection Legislation.
COMPLIANCE 1 The Parties agree that this Agreement formalises the lawful transfer of the Personal Data between the Parties and presents no new or additional privacy concerns.
A data protection impact assessment has been conducted in respect of the Personal Data to be shared and the necessity of the sharing and this Agreement serves to address any residual privacy or information risks and documents the actions taken to identify, address and mitigate those risks wherever possible.
The Parties shall not process the Personal Data in a way that is incompatible with the Agreed Purposes.
As Data Controllers, the Parties remain responsible for ensuring that all uses of the Personal Data are in compliance with all applicable Data Protection Legislation.
This Agreement must be formally approved and signed by the Parties before any Personal Data is shared by persons with authority to do so on behalf of their organisation.
All Parties will ensure that the terms of this Agreement are known and understood by all Staff involved in the sharing of the Personal Data.
Each Party is registered with the ICO has a valid Data Protection Registration Number with its national Data Protection Authority if required by the Enterprise Act 2017 which by the time that the data sharing is expected to commence, covers the intended data sharing pursuant to this Agreement.

PERSONAL DATA 1 The Parties shall, in relation to any of the Personal Data processed in connection with its obligations under this Agreement: 1.1 Process that Personal Data only in accordance with the Schedule, unless the Parties are required to do otherwise by Law, which should be notified to the other Parties (unless notification is prohibited by Law); 1.2 not Process in an irrelevant or excessive way with regard to the Agreed Purposes; 1.3 ensure they do not knowingly or negligently do or omit to do anything which places the Parties breach of the obligations under the Data Protection Legislation; 1.4 ensure that they have in place Protective Measures to protect against: a) unauthorised or unlawful processing of Personal Data; b) accidental loss or destruction of, or damage to, Personal Data, and such measures to be reviewed as appropriate to protect against a Data Loss Event having taken account of the: (i) nature of the data to be protected; (ii) harm that might result from a Data Loss Event; (iii) state of technological development; and (iv) cost of implementing any measures; 1.5 ensure the Personal Data is kept and stored securely in accordance with Data Protection Legislation; 1.6 ensure that: (i) the Staff do not process Personal Data except in accordance the Schedule; (ii) each Party takes all reasonable steps to ensure the reliability and integrity of any Staff who have access to the Personal Data and ensure that they: (A) are aware of and comply with the duties under this Agreement; (B) are subject to appropriate confidentiality undertakings; (C) are informed of the confidential nature of the Personal Data and do not publish, disclose or divulge any of the Personal Data to any third party unless permitted by this Agreement; and (D) have undergone adequate training in the use, care, protection and handling of the Personal Data;
1.7 not transfer the Personal Data outside of the UK unless the prior written consent of all Parties has been obtained and the following conditions are fulfilled: a) the Parties have provided appropriate safeguards in relation to the transfer (whether in accordance with UK GDPR Article 46 or LED Article ; b) the Data Subject has enforceable rights and effective legal remedies; c) the Parties outside the UK comply with the obligations under the Data Protection Legislation by providing an adequate level of protection to any Personal Data that is transferred (or, if it is not so bound, uses its best endeavours to assist the Parties in meeting its obligations under the Data Protection Legislation); 1.8 securely destroy or return the Personal Data (and any copies of it) to the Data Discloser upon termination or expiry of this Agreement unless the Parties are required by Law to retain the Personal Data; and 1.9 as far as is reasonably practical, anonymise or pseudonymise all Personal Data contained in statistics, reports and summaries to minimise the amount of Personal Data shared.
FAIR, LAWFUL AND TRANSPARENT PROCESSING 1 The Personal Data shall be shared in accordance with the Agreed Purposes on the basis of the legal grounds as stated in the Schedule.
The Parties undertake to inform Data Subjects of the purposes for which it will process their Personal Data and provide all of the information that it must provide in accordance with applicable laws, to ensure that the Data Subjects understand how their Personal Data will be processed by the Data Controller.
The Parties shall, in respect of the Personal Data, ensure that their privacy notices are clear and provide sufficient information to Data Subjects, in order for them to understand: 3.1 what Personal Data the Parties are sharing; 3.2 the circumstances in which it will be shared; 3.3 the purposes for the sharing; 3.4 either the identity with whom the Personal Data is shared or a description of the type of organisation that will receive the Personal Data unless an exemption to the transparency provisions applies.
DATA QUALITY 1 The Data Discloser shall ensure that the Personal Data is accurate.
Where either Party becomes aware of inaccuracies in the Personal Data, they will notify the other Party immediately.
DATA SUBJECTS' RIGHTS 1 Data Subjects have the right to obtain certain information about the processing of their Personal Data through a Subject Access Request.
Data Subjects may also request rectification, erasure or blocking of their Personal Data.
The Parties shall maintain a record of Subject Access Requests, the decisions made and any information that was exchanged.
Records must include copies of the request for information, details of the data accessed and shared and where relevant, notes of any meeting, correspondence or phone calls relating to the request, together with relevant dates.
The Parties agree that the responsibility for complying with a Subject Access Request falls to Party receiving the Subject Access Request in respect of the Personal Data held by that Party.
The Parties agree to provide reasonable and prompt assistance (within 5 business days of such a request for assistance) at their own expense as is necessary to each other to enable them to comply with Subject Access Requests and to respond to any other queries or complaints from Data Subjects.
DATA RETENTION AND DELETION 1 The Data Receiver shall not retain or process Personal Data for longer than is necessary to carry out the Agreed Purposes.
Notwithstanding Clause 8.1, the Parties shall continue to retain the Personal Data in accordance with any internal or statutory retention periods applicable in their organisations and/or industry.
The Data Receiver shall ensure that any Personal Data is returned to the Data Discloser or destroyed in the following circumstances: (a) upon termination of the Agreement for whatever reason; (b) upon expiry of the Term (unless extended further in accordance the terms of this Agreement); and (c) once processing of the Personal Data is no longer necessary for the Agreed Purposes.
TRANSFERS 1 For the purposes of this Clause, transfers of Personal Data shall mean any sharing of the Personal Data by the Data Receiver with a third party, and shall include, but is not limited to, the following: a) sharing of the Personal Data with any third party; b) publication of the Personal Data via any medium, including, but not limited to social media, websites, publicly available communications.
storing Personal Data on servers outside the UK; d) subcontracting the processing of Personal Data to data processors located outside the UK; e) granting third parties located outside the UK access rights to the Personal Data.

Where Personal Data is to be shared under clause 9.2, the Data Receiver shall not disclose or transfer the Personal Data outside the UK without first ensuring that adequate and equivalent protections will be afforded to the Personal Data.
SECURITY AND TRAINING 1 The Parties agree to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect the Personal Data in their possession against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, damage, alteration or disclosure, including but not limited to: a) ensuring IT equipment, including portable equipment is kept in lockable areas when unattended; b) not leaving portable equipment containing the Personal Data unattended; c) ensuring Staff use appropriate secure passwords for logging into systems or databases containing the Personal Data; d) ensuring that all IT equipment is protected by antivirus software, firewalls, passwords and suitable encryption devices; e) ensuring that any Special Category Personal Data is stored and transferred (including where stored or transferred on portable devices or removable media) using industry standard 256-bit AES encryption or suitable equivalent; f) limiting access to relevant databases and systems to those Staff who need to have access to the Personal Data, and ensuring that passwords are changed and updated regularly to prevent inappropriate access when individuals are no longer engaged by the Party; g) conducting regular threat assessment or penetration testing on systems; h) ensuring all Staff handling Personal Data have been made aware of their responsibilities with regards to handling of the Personal Data; and i) allowing for inspections and assessments to be undertaken by the other Party in respect of the security measures taken, or producing evidence of those measures if requested.
PERSONAL DATA BREACHES AND REPORTING PROCEDURES 1 The Parties shall ensure they do not knowingly or negligently do or omit to do anything which places either Party in breach of the Data Protection Legislation.
Each Party shall inform the other immediately if it: a) is in breach of the Protective Measures requirements; b) receives a request to rectify, block or erase the Personal Data; c) receives any complaint or communication relating to either Party's obligations under the Data Protection Legislation; d) receives any communication from the Information Commissioner or any other regulatory authority in connection with the Personal Data; e) receives a request from any third party for disclosure of the Personal Data where compliance with such request is required or purported to be required by Law; or f) becomes aware of a Data Loss Event, and without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours, shall notify the other Parties, by: (i) contacting the Data Protection Officer(s) as detailed within the Schedule; and (ii) informing them of the Data Loss Event, including in its notification: (A) a description of the Data Loss Event including where possible, the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects concerned and the categories and approximate number of data records concerned; and (B) a description of the measures taken or proposes to take to address the Data Loss Event, including, where appropriate, measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.
Following the notification of a Data Loss Event, the informing Party shall immediately assess the risks and consequences of the Data Loss Event and take measures, including: (i) to mitigate the possible adverse effects of the Data Loss Event; and (ii) to protect the data and the interests/safety of the Data Subject and/or Data Controller, and inform the other Parties as to the measures that it has taken.
The informing Party’s obligation to notify shall include the provision of further information in phases, as details become available.
RESOLUTION OF DISPUTES WITH DATA SUBJECTS OR THE DATA 1 Taking into account the nature of the processing, the Parties shall provide each other, at their own expense, with full assistance in relation to the Personal Data and the obligations under Data Protection Legislation and any complaint, communication or request made including by promptly providing (and in any event within 24 hours of being received): i. full details and copies of the complaint, communication or request; ii. such assistance as is reasonably requested to enable the Parties to comply with a Data Subject Access Request within the relevant timescales set out in the Data Protection Legislation; iii. assistance as required with respect to any request from the Information Commissioner’s Office, or any consultation by the Parties with the Information Commissioner's Office; iv. any Personal Data it holds in relation to a Data Subject (as appropriate) within the timescales required by the Council; and v. each other Party with any information requested in relation to the request.

Legal Basis for sharing and Privacy notice requirements Personal data: Article 6(1) (c) legal duty and 6(1)(e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest .
The Crime and Disorder Act 1998 gives each LA in England & Wales has the responsibility to formulate a strategy to reduce crime and disorder in their area and to work with police authorities to do this.
Section 115 of the Act gives partners the power to share information for the purpose of reducing crime and disorder.
Schedule 9 (5) of the Police and Justice Act strengthens this by introducing a new duty on the same agencies.
The Children Act 1989 Under S.47 of the Children’s Act 1989, a Local Authority has a duty to investigate when informed that a child in their area is in police protection or the subject of a protection order.
The Children Act 2004, as amended by the Children and Social Work Act 2017, places duties on key agencies in a local area.
Specifically, the police, clinical commissioning groups and the local authority are under a duty to make arrangements to work together, and with other partners locally, to safeguard and promote the welfare of all children in their area.
The Statutory Guidance - Working Together to Safeguard Children (2018) and Information Sharing – Advice to Practitioners providing safeguarding services to Children, Young People, Parents, and Carers (July 2018) emphasise the need to share appropriately.
Special Category Data: Art 9(g) Reasons of substantial public interest (with a basis in law) and (h) Health or social care (with a basis in law).
For art 9(g) the Data Protection Act 2018 Schedule 1 Part 2 conditions are para 6 6 (Statutory and government purposes) and para 18 (Safeguarding of children and of individuals at risk) with the underlying law being that identified in art 6 above; and for art 9(h) the Data Protection Act 2018 Schedule 1 Part 1 condition is (2)(2)(e) Health or social care purposes, the provision of social care.
The underlying law is the same as for art 6.

Section 3: Explanations and Conclusions Practitioners will pull together and analyse elements of the Information Gathering section to make judgements about the young person in four main areas: explaining offending behaviour patterns and trends based on the young person’s individual context considering potential future behaviour, its likelihood and impact use 1 and 2 alongside an indicative rating of reoffending to determine the likelihood of reoffending rating and Risk of Serious Harm rating making a professional judgement about the likelihood and impact of adverse outcomes in relation to the young person’s safety and well-being
