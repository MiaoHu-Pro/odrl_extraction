Purpose of the Data Sharing 1 Framework This Data Sharing Agreement sets out the framework for the sharing of personal data between the Parties and defines the principles and procedures that the Parties shall adhere to and the responsibilities the Parties owe to one another.
This agreement is required to ensure that any sharing of personal data is carried out in accordance with the GDPR and the Data Sharing and Governance Act 2019, and each Party agrees to be bound by this agreement until such time as the agreement is terminated, or the Party withdraws from the agreement.
The Parties shall not process shared personal data in a way that is incompatible with the relevant purposes and this agreement.
The Parties will ensure that the Data Sharing Agreement remains fit for purpose, accurate and up to date.
The Parties will actively monitor and periodically review the data sharing arrangement to ensure that it continues to be compliant with data protection law, that it continues to meet its objective, that safeguards continue to match any risks posed, that records are accurate and up to date, that there is adherence to the data retention period agreed and that an appropriate level of data security is maintained.
The Parties must address all recommendations made regarding this Data Sharing Agreement by the Data Governance Board.

Dept. of Justice, IIP The function of the IIP Unit, Immigration Service Delivery, Department of Justice is to administer the Immigrant Investor Programme.
The Immigrant Investor Programme (IIP) enables non-EEA nationals to secure immigration permission to Ireland on the basis of long-term investment of a sum of money in a project that has been approved for that purpose by the Department of Justice and other Government stakeholders.
Office of the Revenue Commissioners The function of the Exchange of Information (EOI) Branch, Office of the Revenue Commissioners, is to exchange relevant information with tax administrations in other jurisdictions in accordance with Revenue’s commitments under international agreements such as the CRS.
The CRS is the global standard for the Automatic Exchange Of Information (AEOI) regarding financial accounts between tax authorities.
The EOI Branch spontaneously exchanges relevant information relating to successful IIP applicants with their jurisdiction of tax residence under relevant legal instruments.
This allows Ireland to meet its commitments under the CRS, maintaining its integrity and effectiveness.

[Revenue Commissioner s Once the data relating to approved IIP applicants is received by Revenue from the Department of Justice through encrypted email, the records are securely and appropriately stored in the computer infrastructure of the Exchange of Information (EOI) Branch.
The data is reviewed by a member of staff of the EOI Branch to ensure that it includes the details specified at section 3.4 of the Data Sharing Agreement and does not contain any details other than the information to be disclosed under said agreement.
Once verification has been completed, the data is organised by a member of staff of the EOI Branch in accordance with the jurisdiction of tax residence of the approved IIP applicants.
In addition, the jurisdiction-specific files are reviewed by a Competent Authority of the EOI Branch.
Specify any further processing by the Party or Parties receiving data of the personal data disclosed by the disclosing body under this Data Sharing Agreement.
[Revenue Commissioner s] In order to ensure that Ireland meets its commitments under the OECD Common Reporting Standard, the personal details of approved IIP applicants are spontaneously exchanged with the applicant’s jurisdiction of tax residence.
Such spontaneous exchanges take place under the provisions of Ireland’s Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs) or by way of the Multilateral Convention for Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters (MAAC).
Spontaneous exchanges are prepared by a member of staff of the EOI Branch and reviewed by the Competent Authority.
In accordance with the provisions of the relevant DTA or MAAC, each spontaneous exchange is made by the Irish Competent Authority to the partner jurisdiction’s Competent Authority.
All information spontaneously exchanged is clearly stamped to indicate the use and disclosure permitted by the relevant DTA or the MAAC before it is sent to the partner jurisdiction.
Spontaneous exchanges may take place via digitally secure encrypted means or sent to a partner jurisdiction’s Competent Authority by registered post.

Withdrawal from Agreement 1 Procedure Each Party commits to giving a minimum of 90 days’ notice of its intention to withdraw from or terminate this Data Sharing Agreement.
Each Party disclosing personal data pursuant to this Agreement reserves the right to withdraw, without notice, access to such data where that Party has reason to believe the conditions of this Data Sharing Agreement are not being observed.
Each Party disclosing data will accept no responsibility for any consequences arising from the exercise of this right.
Where the disclosing Party is subsequently satisfied that the conditions of the Data Sharing Agreement are being observed, access will be restored forthwith.
Where access to shared personal data is withdrawn, the withdrawing Party shall provide to the other Party reasons for that withdrawal as soon as is practicable thereafter.
Where there are only 2 Parties, withdrawal by either one shall be considered a termination of the agreement.
Where an agreement has multiple Parties and one withdraws, the Lead Agency should update the schedule and inform the other Parties to the agreement.
Where a Data Sharing Agreement expires or is terminated, the Lead Agency shall notify the Minister in writing within 10 days of the withdrawal.
The Lead Agency shall also notify the Data Governance Board as soon as practicable after such expiration or termination, as the case may be.

Necessary for the Performance of a Function Outline the reasons why the disclosure of information under this agreement is necessary for the performance of the relevant function and explain why it is proportionate in that context.
The Department of Justice (Immigration Service Delivery) administers the Immigrant Investor Programme (IIP) scheme, which enables non-EEA nationals to secure immigration permission to Ireland on the basis of long-term investment of a sum of money in a project that has been approved for that purpose by the Department of Justice and other Government stakeholders.
In order to ensure that Ireland meets its commitments under the OECD Common Reporting Standard, limited relevant personal details of approved IIP applicants are disclosed to the Office of the Revenue Commissioners, who in turn exchange the information with appropriate international tax authorities.
It is proportionate that this information is disclosed in order to ensure that the Immigrant Investor Programme is fully compliant with all relevant financial and tax obligations and commitments to international agreements and is not misused in any way.
This ensures that the public can continue to have confidence in the scheme.
