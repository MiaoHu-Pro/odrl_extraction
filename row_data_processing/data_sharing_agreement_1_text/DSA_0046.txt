1. BACKGROUND
1.1 The Early Help data collection is aiming to collect data on all families worked with by
early help services in all 152 upper tier local authorities in England bi-annually to better
understand issues faced by families and the long-lasting positive change they have
achieved.
1.2 Insights from this research will enable central government and local authorities to
resource and commission support more effectively and target specialist support at
specific problems (thereby increasing the impact and value for money of existing
programmes) and provide essential inputs to future policy discussions on the shape of
early help and children’s social care and the response to the Care Review.
1.3 This will be the first collection of its kind for Early Help cases.
1.3.1 The collection will include all cases with an Early Help assessment, as well
as Children’s Social Care cases if they have been successfully claimed
under the Supporting Families programme.
1.4 The data will be used by local authorities and central government to better understand;
1.4.1 the problems families are facing
1.4.2 which services are providing support to families
1.4.3 whether long lasting positive change has been achieved across those
problems.
1.5 This insight will enable central government and LAs to resource and commission support
more effectively, identify and share evidence of good practice and target specialist
support towards specific problems.

1.6 A DPIA has been carried out, which covers the data sharing process and reviewed by
the KIA team.
1.7 This Agreement exists to provide a framework for compliance with UK data protection
provisions and the Human Rights Act 1998, including the data protection principles at
article 5 of the UK GDPR, and taking account of the Information Commissioner’s Code of
Practice on Data Sharing.

4. PURPOSE OF RESEARCH AND USE OF PERSONAL DATA

4.1 Data Source:
The data (family level and cohort level) will be provided by local authorities (LAs) from their
own case management systems. This will be data collected from families and partners, to
provide a holistic picture of all the issues faced by families supported in Early Help and/or
Children’s Social Care (if the case is claimed under the Supporting Families payments by
results process). The LA family identifier (Family ID) will be used to create pseudo-
anonymised data.
4.1.1 Collecting the data with a family identifier is necessary in order to identify which
problems are correlated with one another and associated with outcomes. Without this
identifier it would also not be possible to track trends over time (problems, outcomes,
re-referrals).
4.1.2 However, in the interest of data minimisation, the LA family identifier (Family ID) will
be replaced by a new Family ID created by IFF before being shared back to DLUHC
for government analytical use. This will minimise the sharing of personal data.
4.2 How we will collect and use the data:
DLUHC will ask the IFF Research to collect these data from local authorities via their secure
data transfer portal. This portal is encrypted, and password protected. This portal will be
subject to a full cyber assurance check by the DLUHC cyber security.
4.2.1 IFF Research will store the data.
4.2.2 IFF Research will perform data validations and analysis and provide reports back to
the relevant local authority, and national reports to the national Supporting Families
team. These reports will only contain cohort level data which is therefore non-
identifiable or personal data.
4.2.3 See data flow in clause 7 for full details.
4.3 How the data will be shared:
4.3.1 The processed data file data will be provided back to DLUHC by IFF using Egress, a
DLUHC approved secure file-sharing software.
4.3.2 DLUHC will share the processed data with a small number of analyst colleagues
within the Department for Education (DfE) as the Supporting Families programme is
a shared programme. The terms of this transfer with DfE are covered by a data
processing agreement (MOU). This data will not include the original Family ID
provided by LAs, and therefore will be fully anonymised.
4.3.3 When the processed data files are shared with DfE colleagues this will be via a
secure, encrypted, password protected platform (DLUHC SharePoint).
4.3.4 The Supplier generated local and national reports produced will be shared with
DLUHC. DLUHC will then share the relevant local and national reports back to the
local authorities. Additionally, DLUHC will share the national with other relevant

government departments (DWP, DfE, DHSC, MoJ). The unique identifier/Family ID
will not be able to be accessed through these reports and therefore there is no
personal identifiable data contained within these reports. Therefore, they will be
shared via email.
5. PURPOSE OF DATA SHARING
5.1 This will be the first collection of its kind for Early Help cases. The data will be used by
local authorities and central government to better understand; a) the problems families are
facing, b) which services are providing support to families and c) whether long lasting
positive change has been achieved across those problems. This insight will enable central
government and LAs to resource and commission support more effectively, identify and
share evidence of good practice and target specialist support towards specific problems.
5.2 The project will involve processing and linking data on vulnerable families and children,
but DLUHC will not have any direct relationship with these individuals.
5.2.1 The purpose of the data collection is to capture the problems and outcomes that
families experience who are worked with by early help services. The data will deliver
insights into what particular services are supporting families and whether long lasting
positive change has been achieved across these problems.
5.3 The use of data in this way will be justified by both public interest for the general
processing and the substantial public interest in protecting vulnerable children and
families (special category data). This is in line with Article 6(1)(e) and Article 9(2)(g) of
UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) on the basis of Union or Member State
law which shall be proportionate to the aim pursued, respect the essence of the right to
data protection and provide for suitable and specific measures to safeguard the
fundamental rights and the interests of the data subject.
5.3.1 The insight generated from the data will enable central government and local
authorities to resource and commission support more effectively, identify and share
evidence of good practice and target specialist support at specific problems. This will
lead to an improved service for individuals and families, and families receiving the
right support at the right time.
6. LAWFUL BASIS FOR PROCESSING THE DATA
6.1 The following are the lawful bases in data protection legislation under which DLUHC will
process the personal data (including special categories of personal data):
6.1.1 Article 6(1)(e) of the UK GDPR - the processing is necessary for DLUHC to
perform a task in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority. In
particular, to help further the understanding of needs identified for families within
Early Help, identify the scale of re-referrals and be able to share insight on a
national and local level to support the work in improving the lives of families.
6.1.1.1 In order to rely on article 6(1)(e), article 6(3) requires there to be a
separate legal basis laid down in law to justify the processing. DLUHC
is relying on its common law powers to justify this processing: the

Secretary of State for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities relies on
the common law powers of the Crown as the separate legal basis for
this processing.
6.1.2 Article 9(2)(g) of the UK GDPR – the processing is necessary for reasons of
substantial public interest. DLUHC will rely on meeting the condition in Schedule
1, Part 2, paragraph 6 of the DPA 2018 to process special category personal
data in accordance with section 10(3) of the DPA 2018.
6.1.2.1 In order to track re-referral rates i.e., families who receive multiple
interventions, the pseudo-anonymised Family ID needs to be supplied.
6.1.2.2 No other personal data is required to be shared so minimises the risk
although the data still requires appropriate security and handling as
personal data.
6.1.3 DLUHC will also meet the conditions in article 10 of the UK GDPR and section
10(5) of the DPA 2018 to process criminal offence data since processing is
authorised under Section 10(5) and Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 6 of the DPA
2018.
6.1.3.1 Data relating to criminal convictions or offences will also be processed,
for which we refer to Article 10 of GDPR. However, this will again be
pseudo-anonymised non-identifiable and minimal (e.g., the presence or
absence of criminal conviction within the family).
6.2 The data protection lawful bases and legal powers under which IFF Research will
process the personal data (including special categories of personal data) are contained
within the Contract between DLUHC and the Supplier.
6.2.1 Schedule 11 of this contract lays out the;
6.2.1.1 Identity of data controllers and data processors
6.2.1.2 The duration of processing

6.2.1.4 The type of personal data
6.2.1.5 The categories of data subjects
6.2.1.6 Plans for return and destruction of fata one processing is complete
6.2.1.7 Confirmation that all data will only be processed within the United
Kingdom.
6.2.2 These data processing arrangements are also covered by a full DPIA which has
been reviewed by the DLUHC KIA team.

8. PERSONAL DATA COVERED BY THIS AGREEMENT
A unique identifier referenced as a ‘Family ID’ is being supplied with the data and so
represents pseudonymised personal data. If local authority systems were compromised, and
Family IDs matched with personal information held by local authorities, there is a risk
families could be identified. This is therefore considered to be personal data because the
data in the family ‘packet’ contains details relating to family needs, which includes sensitive
topics such as domestic abuse and childhood sexual exploitation. However, we have
minimised this risk by creating a new unique identifier/Family ID for government analytical
use. This means the pseudonymised personal data is not shared any further than is
necessary and therefore in adherence to the principle of data minimisation.
9. PROTECTION OF PERSONAL DATA
9.1 DLUHC and Camden Council agree to share the Personal Data only as set out in this
Agreement.
9.2 In relation to any third-party Processors appointed by the Parties to process the shared
Personal Data, the Parties will comply with data protection legislation in relation to that
appointment. In this case, the third-party Processors are small number of DfE analyst
colleagues and IFF Research.
9.3 DLUHC and Camden Council will maintain the levels of security under this Agreement
throughout the process.
9.4 DLUHC and Camden Council shall not disclose or transfer data outside the UK.
9.5. DLUHC and Camden Council will each ensure that all staff involved in the processing of
personal data under this Agreement have received appropriate data protection training.
10. RETENTION AND DESTRUCTION OF DATA
The data will be retained by the Supplier throughout the duration of the project (until March
2025). This is needed in order to identify trends that emerge over time and successfully
complete valuable analysis. DLUHC will retain non-personal data for further analytical use,
however this data will not contain the Family ID and therefore will be fully anonymised. No
personal data will be retained beyond March 2025 by DLUHC, DfE or IFF Research.
11. LOSS OR UNAUTHORISED RELEASE
11.1 DLUHC and Camden Council (including the Supplier, IFF Research) will report to all
parties any loss or unauthorised release of the personal data no later than 48 hours after the
loss or unauthorised release is identified (comes to their attention). In the case of a personal
data breach, the data controller shall without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than
72 hours after having become aware of it, notify the personal data breach to the Information
Commissioner’s Office (as supervisory authority competent in accordance with article 55 UK
GDPR), unless the personal data breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and
freedoms of natural persons. Where the notification to the supervisory authority is not made
within 72 hours, it shall be accompanied by reasons for the delay.
11.2 DLUHC and Camden Council acknowledge that any loss or unauthorised release of the
personal data can be treated as valid grounds for immediately terminating this agreement by
any party.

13. RIGHTS OF DATA SUBJECTS
13.1. DLUHC and Camden Council as independent data controllers will ensure that the
processing of the shared personal data is fair and transparent and the data subjects’ right to
be informed under the data protection legislation is met. Data subjects are already informed
that data will be used for evaluation. However, we ask local authorities to update their
privacy notices (held on websites and provided by workers to families on request) to explain
that this will be shared with the national team on a pseudonymised basis.
• We previously did this for the National Impact Study evaluation, so there is
precedent, and areas have taken part in a similar exercise before.
• This is in line with the right to be informed set out in Article 12(1), and Articles 13 and
14 of the GDPR.
13.2. Data subjects have the rights of access accorded by article 15 of the UK GDPR,
including the right to obtain a copy of the information held about them. DLUHC and Camden
Council as data controllers will each be responsible for responding to subject access
requests, under their respective policies and procedures, that each receives. DLUHC and
Camden Council will aim to respond appropriately to any subject access or other rights
request within one month of receipt. If the request is more complex, DLUHC and Camden
Council will respond to rights requests within the appropriate legislative timeframe.
