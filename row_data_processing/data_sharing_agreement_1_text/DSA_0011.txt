2. Why is the information being shared?
Rescue and Response is a three-year MOPAC (Mayor’s Office for Policing
and Crime) funded project, running between 2019 and 2021, which will
support young Londoners affected by cross-border county lines activity.
The signatories to this ISA has responsibilities to share information under
legislation, which includes preventing and detecting crime and/or
safeguarding and supporting individuals.
The sharing of information is necessary for the Rescue and Response project
to develop and deliver a support service for young people who are vulnerable
and caught up in county lines drug distribution networks across London, and
identify and use enforcement against those exploiting young people. This will
benefit the public and community by reducing crime and improving the
potential outcomes of young people.
Everyone who works with children has a responsibility for keeping them safe.
No single practitioner or agency can have a full picture of a child’s needs and
circumstances and, if children and families are to receive the right help at the
2

right time, everyone who comes into contact with them has a role to play in
identifying concerns, sharing information and taking prompt action.
Sharing information is relevant for
 Addressing the increase in number of Young people identified as
being exploited/at risk of count lines exploitation
 Increased engagement with services for the young people referred
and engaged.
 Better understanding of County Lines and peer and criminal
networks associated.
 Increased feelings of safety and reduced feelings of vulnerability of
those young people engaged through the project.
 Identification of a person’s associations to ascertain if they are
linked to/at risk from a criminal network
 Identification of a person’s aliases to clarify if they are linked to/at
risk from a criminal network
 Identification of a criminal network’s to identify young people that
may be at risk from their criminal activity
 The prevention of crime and disorder
 The detection of Crime
 The protection of vulnerable members of the community
3. Potential recipients, types of recipients and the circumstances they will have
access (if information is shared further)?
Disclosing organisations will share with other signatory boroughs and
police any relevant information regarding the young person on a need to
know basis. This will include intelligence to prevent further crimes and to
safeguard children and young people.
The main information sharing will take place between signatory agencies,
with the Rescue & Response Project Team acting as the hub, and
delivering support services. The hub will be a central control for sharing
information about individuals. It will manage the sharing of information as
referrals from and to other signatories.
Disclosing organisations may share with other internal and external
departments so the organisation can provide a more holistic service for
their benefit. This will be under existing sharing frameworks within each
organisation as data controllers.
4. What information being shared?
Personal and special category personal data will be processed. The data
items include:

 criminal data
 health data (mental health)
 financial data (mainly benefits),
 contact data
 family data
 local authority service data (education, children safeguarding, adult
safeguarding).
The data subjects will be victims/potential victims and alleged/convicted
perpetrators, as well as individuals who are connected to those data subjects,
such as family, witnesses and providers of support. The relationships or
identity (i.e. victim or perpetrator) will be defined and flagged during
processing.

 Care Act 2014 – relevant sections include s.1 and s.6
The R&R Project will also cover young people aged up to 25 years. The
Care Act allows the sharing of adult information with care and support
needs to keep them safe from abuse and neglect. It is aimed at people
with care and support needs who may be in vulnerable circumstances and
at risk of abuse or neglect. In these cases, local services must work
together to spot those at risk and take steps to protect them. The Act aims
to create a legal framework so key organisations and individuals with
responsibility for Adult Safeguarding can agree on how they must work
together and what roles they must play to keep adults at risk safe.
There are additional duties in the Care and Support Statutory Guidance
2018, which sits beneath Care Act.
 Anti-Social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 – relevant
sections include those under Part 4, Part 5 and Part 9
This Act provides a framework to address criminal activity which falls
under anti-social behaviour to provide better protection for victims and
communities.
 Crime & Disorder Act 1998– relevant sections include s.115
The Act makes provision for preventing crime and disorder, this gives
power to disclose information to specified relevant authorities where the
disclosure is necessary or expedient for the purposes of the Act.
Statutory guidance:
 Pan London Child and Adult Protection Procedures
Provide statutory duties which allow organisations to process and share
information to better safeguard vulnerable adults and children through a
multi-agency approach.
 Working Together to Safeguard Children 2018
This is statutory guidance that allows the sharing of adult data for the
benefit of the child as well as the sharing of children’s data. It states that
local authorities, working with partner organisations and agencies, have
specific duties to safeguard and promote the welfare of all children in their
area. Children may be vulnerable to neglect and abuse or exploitation from
within their family and from individuals they come across in their day-to-
day lives. These threats can take a variety of different forms, including:
sexual, physical and emotional abuse; neglect; exploitation by criminal
gangs and organised crime groups; trafficking; online abuse; sexual
exploitation and the influences of extremism leading to radicalisation.
Whatever the form of abuse or neglect, practitioners should put the needs
of children first when determining what action to take.

15. What are the procedures for dealing with a breach of the agreement – notifying
and investigating breaches
The Brent form will be used and sent to the other effected data controller and
sent within 24 hours to relevant effected parties
All signatories will maintain robust data breach detection, investigation and
internal reporting procedures and follow the guidance of the Information
Commissioner’s Office (ICO).
If a signatory has a data breach (or suspects a data breach in another
signatory organisation) which relates to the information being shared under
this agreement, it will:
 inform the parties affected by the breach as soon as there is
awareness of a breach and Brent Rescue and Response Project Team
- immediately identifying any urgent remedial actions required that
might affect other parties
 liaise with the other parties as appropriate to take steps to establish the
likelihood and severity of the resulting risk to affected data subjects
including the risk of physical, material or non-material damage to
individuals such as loss of control over their personal data or limitation
of their rights, discrimination, identity theft or fraud, financial loss,
unauthorised reversal of pseudonymisation, damage to reputation, loss
of confidentiality of personal data protected by professional secrecy or
any other significant economic or social disadvantage to the natural
person concerned.
 identify whether it is likely that there will be such a risk to data subject’s
rights and freedoms, and notify all affected Parties
 where the incident meets the criteria, notify the ICO (within 72 hours
where feasible) and, where appropriate, inform those individuals
affected without undue delay.
In all cases the signatory will keep full written records of any data breach, its
investigation, the remedial steps taken and the reasons for such steps.
All parties affected by a data breach will share with other signatories a
lessons learned analysis of the cause and effects of reportable data breaches
and will share with all partners at the next R&R Strategic Governance Group.
