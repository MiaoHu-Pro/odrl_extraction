1. Introduction
1.1. This Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on personal data
sharing is not legally binding and no legal obligations or legal rights shall
arise between the Parties from the provisions of this MoU (hereafter
referred to as “this Agreement”). By signing this MoU (i) the Parties do
not intend to create contractual or other legal relations; and (ii) the
Parties agree to adhere to the framework set out in the Agreement.
1.2. It has been agreed between DLUHC and Local Authorities to
ensure agreement and appropriate arrangements are in place in respect
of the sharing of Personal Data as Data Controllers.
1.3. The use of Personal Data governed by this data sharing
Agreement (DSA) covers the processing of Personal Data provided by
DLUHC, to be transferred to and by London Borough of Camden for the
purpose set out in clause 4 below. The transfer and use of Personal Data
is necessary to deliver the Communities for Afghans project (CfA).
1.4. Given urgency for the sharing of Personal Data covered in this
DSA, a Party accessing or otherwise using the data constitutes formal
agreement to the terms of this DSA, in parallel to the execution clause
29 below.
2. Roles and Responsibilities
2.1. DLUHC is an Independent Data Controller for the processing of the
Personal Data up to the point that the Personal Data is provided to
London Borough of Camden.
2.2. Citizens UK is the main Data Processor contracted by DLUHC for
processing of its Personal Data. It will directly receive Personal Data
from London Borough of Camden and other parties delivering CfA. For
avoidance of doubt, as DLUHC’s Processor any direct sharing of
Personal Data with Citizens UK is synonymous with sharing with
DLUHC; DLUHC will be solely responsible for the purpose, use and
security of the Personal Data from the point that Citizens UK receives it
from London Borough of Camden.
2.3. London Borough of Camden is an Independent Data Controller
from the point that Personal Data is provided to them by DLUHC to
facilitate delivery of CfA, and/or it collects its own personal data for the
purpose of delivering the project. For avoidance of doubt, London
Borough of Camden of the Personal Data from this point of receipt from
DLUHC. Similarly, where London Borough of Camden provides
Personal Data to DLUHC; DLUHC will be solely responsible for the
purpose, use and security of the Personal Data from this point of receipt
from London Borough of Camden.
2.4. No Controller-Processor, or Joint Data Controller relationship will
apply.

5. Legal basis for the use of the data
5.1. The legal basis that DLUHC and the Local Authority are using for
the sharing and use of this data is
5.1.1. UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) – the processing is necessary for the
performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the
exercise of official authority vested in the Controller,
5.1.2. DPA 2018, Part 2, Chapter 2, section 8 (d) – the exercise of a
function of the Crown, a Minister of the Crown or a government
department.
5.2. For DLUHC the vires for use of the above is section 59(2)(c) of
the Nationality, Immigration and Asylum Act 2002.
5.3. DLUHC’s view is that the most suitable vires for Local Authority
may be section 111 of the Local Government Act 1972, though if you
have previously worked directly with Home Office on refugee matters
you may have your vires for processing identified already. The Local
Authority is an Independent Data Controller and you may wish to take
your own legal advice on your legal powers to process the shared
Personal Data after receipt.
6. Special category data being processed
6.1. Special category data is to be shared between the Parties and is
set out in Annex 1 of this DSA.
6.2. The legal basis that DLUHC is using for the sharing and use of
this Personal Data is
6.2.1. UK GDPR Article 9(2)(g) – processing is necessary for
reasons of substantial public interest, on the basis of domestic
law which shall be proportionate to the aim pursued, respect the
essence of the right to data protection and provide for suitable
and specific measures to safeguard the fundamental rights and
the interests of the data subject;
6.2.2. DPA 2018, Schedule 1, Part 2, section 6 – the exercise of
a function of the Crown, a Minister of the Crown or a government
department.
6.3. For DLUHC the vires for use of the above identical to clause 5.2
above.
6.4. For the Local Authority, the vires for the use of the above is
identical to clause 5.3 above.

10. Compliance with the Data Protection Principles
10.1. Save to the extent that an exemption applies to limit or exclude
any of them, compliance with the Data Protection Legislation shall
include (but is not limited to) compliance with the Data Protection
Principles as described in Article 5 of the UK GDPR.
10.2. Personal Data shall therefore be –
10.2.1. processed lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner
(‘lawfulness, fairness and transparency’);
10.2.2. collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and
not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those
purposes (‘purpose limitation’);
10.2.3. adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in
relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data
minimisation’);
10.2.4. accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date and that
every reasonable step is taken to ensure that personal data that
are inaccurate, having regard to the purposes for which they are
processed, are erased or rectified without delay (‘accuracy’);
10.2.5. kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects
for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the
personal data are processed (‘storage limitation’);
10.2.6. processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security
of the personal data, including protection against unauthorised or
unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or
damage, using appropriate technical or organisational measures
(‘integrity and confidentiality’).
10.2.7. Processed in accordance with the UK GDPR and
demonstrating our compliance.
11. Protective Measures and Personal Data Breaches
11.1. The parties undertake to have in place throughout the Term
appropriate technical and organisational security measures to:

to notify any Supervisory Authority or data subject(s).
11.5. The Parties agree to provide reasonable assistance as is
necessary to each other to facilitate the handling of any Personal Data
Breach in an expeditious and compliant manner.
12. Data Subject Rights
12.1. The Parties will comply in full with the rights of Data Subjects in
Chapter III of the UK GDPR where applicable.
12.2. Save where they are limited or excluded by an exemption these
may include the right to –
12.2.1. Object – the right of the Data Subject to request that their
Personal Data is not processed for certain purposes.
12.2.2. Erasure - the right of the Data Subject to request that their
Personal Data is erased where one of the statutory grounds
applies.

12.2.3. Data portability – the right of the Data Subject to obtain
Personal Data and reuse their Personal Data for their own
purposes across different services in certain circumstances.
12.2.4. Restrict processing - the right of the Data Subject to
request that the processing of their Personal Data is restricted in
certain circumstances – for example, where accuracy is
contested.
12.2.5. Rectification - the right of the Data Subject to request that
any inaccuracies in their Personal Data are rectified without
delay. Request that any incomplete personal data is completed,
including by means of a supplementary statement.
12.2.6. Access - the right of the Data Subject to request access to
information about how their Personal Data is processed and to
request a copy of that personal data.
13. Data Protection Breach
13.1. Each Party shall notify the other(s) as soon as possible or within
24 hours (whichever is sooner) of becoming aware of a Data Protection
Breach in respect of Personal Data used under this Agreement.
13.2. The Parties agree that where appropriate, they will work together
and ensure co-operation regarding the handling of such a breach.
14. Integrity and training of Personnel
14.1. The Parties will ensure that they take all reasonable steps to
ensure the reliability and integrity of its Personnel who have access to
the Personal Data which is processed under this DSA that they are
aware of their obligations under the Data Protection Legislation and in
particular that they have had appropriate training on the handling of
Personal Data.
15. International Transfers of Data
15.1. The Parties are aware of the prohibition in Article 44 of the UK
GDPR against the transfer of Personal Data used under this DSA to
‘third countries’ (as described in that Article) unless certain conditions
are met. In the event that either is in any doubt about the legality of an
international transfer of the personal data processed under this
agreement, it shall consult the other as appropriate.
16. Additional notification requirements

in relation to the personal data processed under this Agreement, it –
16.1.1. receives any communication from the Information
Commissioner or any other regulatory authority;
16.1.2. receives a letter before action or other communication
indicating an intention to commence legal proceedings in respect
of Personal Data processed under this DSA;

20.1. The Parties’ Privacy Notices shall reflect the data processing

21.1. The Parties shall maintain complete and accurate processing of
records to demonstrate their compliance with the Data Protection
Legislation in relation to the Personal Data processed under this

22. Retention and Deletion of Personal Data
22.1. In accordance with the principle of data minimisation the Parties
shall delete their relevant personal data as required by the records
retention schedules and within 2 years of the closure of the scheme
unless DLUHC identifies that its continued retention is unnecessary
before that point.
22.2. The Parties shall not retain or process Personal Data for longer
than is necessary to carry out the Agreed Purposes. This includes any
statutory, legal or professional retention periods applicable to the
personal data.
23. Term and Termination
23.1. This Agreement shall commence on 01 July 2024 and will run for
6 months from this date unless terminated earlier in accordance with
clause 23.2.

23.2. This Agreement can be terminated before its end date as per
clause 23.1 only via mutual agreement between both Parties, with at
least three months' notice in writing, unless both Parties are agreed that

24. Resolution of disputes with data subjects or the Supervisory Authority
24.1. If either of the Parties has any issues, concerns or complaints
about this Agreement it shall notify the other and they shall, acting in
good faith, seek to resolve the issue by negotiations between
themselves, and follow the resolution process.
24.2. In the event of a dispute or claim brought by a data subject or the
Supervisory Authority concerning the processing of Personal Data
against either or both Parties, the Parties will inform each other about
any such disputes or claims and will co-operate with a view to settling
them amicably in a timely fashion.
24.3. The Parties agree to respond to any generally available non-
binding mediation procedure initiated by a data subject or by the
Supervisory Authority. If they do participate in the proceedings, the
parties may elect to do so remotely (such as by telephone or other
electronic means). The Parties also agree to consider participating in
any other arbitration, mediation or other dispute resolution proceedings
developed for data protection disputes.
24.4. Each Party shall abide by a decision of a competent court in
England and Wales or of that Supervisory Authority.
25. Resolution of disputes between parties to this agreement
25.1. The Parties shall each appoint a nominated representative to
whom any disputes arising from the operation of this Agreement shall be
referred.
25.2. If the Parties are unable to agree a matter arising under the terms
and clauses of this Agreement, such a dispute shall be referred to the
senior officers of the nominated representatives for resolution. The
decision of the senior officers shall bind all Parties and be treated as
resolving the dispute.
25.3. The Parties shall, where a dispute cannot be resolved by
agreement between their senior officers, appoint an adjudicator whose
decision shall be final.
25.4. In the event of a dispute or claim brought by a data subject or the
Information Commissioner’s Office concerning the processing of shared
Personal Data against either of the Parties, the Parties will inform each
other about any such disputes or claims and will cooperate with a view
to settling them amicably in a timely fashion.
