INFORMATION SHARING PROCEDURE
INTRODUCTION
1. This is a purpose-specific Information Sharing Procedure made under the
Overarching Information Sharing Protocol between the Greater London Authority
(GLA) and London Borough of Camden.
2. This Procedure shall be entered into by the specific business units/legal entities
within the GLA and London Borough of Camden that are responsible for the purpose
specific activity to which the information sharing relates.
EFFECTIVE DATE
3. 1st April 2022
DESIGNATED POINTS OF CONTACT
4. GLA Private Rented Sector Programme Manager (Housing and Land),
GLA Senior Projects Officer (Housing and Land), GLA Projects Officer (Housing
and Land).
5. Primary contact: Operations Managers (Enforcement team and Rogue Landlord team)
Secondary contact: Principal EHO (Enforcement team)
PURPOSE AND BENEFITS
Purpose
6. The Mayor has committed to creating a London Rogue Landlord and Agent Checker
(RLAC) on the GLA website, containing details of landlords and letting agents who
have acted unlawfully and thus undergone enforcement action by participating
partners (London boroughs or the London Fire Brigade).
7. A limited range of details will be accessible to the public, and a wider range, which
will include historic convictions and other enforcement activity, to participating
partners and the GLA. The data sharing will be a one-way transfer of data relating
to landlord and letting agent enforcement action from the participating partner to the
GLA.
8. There are effectively three tiers of user rights to the RLAC. The first is open to the
general public on the GLA website, the second is “read-only” access for participating
partners to allow officers to view additional enforcement actions and historic
convictions, and the third provides upload and editing rights for a small number of
officers from the participating partner organisation.
Benefits
9. The benefits of the RLAC can be outlined according to the following beneficiaries:

9.1. GLA
It will enable the Mayor to send a clear message to landlords and letting agents
acting unlawfully across London that successful enforcement actions for relevant
offences will result in them being named publicly, making it harder for them to
operate ‘under the radar’.
9.2. Participating partners
It will support London boroughs and the London Fire Brigade in their enforcement
efforts through information sharing and increased awareness amongst renters,
landlords and letting agents of the work that boroughs are doing locally to crack
down on criminal behaviour.
9.3. General public
It will provide renters with a readily accessible means of checking whether a landlord
or agent they are considering renting a property from has faced enforcement action
for a housing related offence. It will seek to ensure the private rented housing
market operates in a fairer and more transparent way, and that tenants are
protected from exploitation by unscrupulous landlords and letting agents. The
Report a Rogue Tool sits alongside the RLAC to make reporting a landlord or agent
to their borough clearer and easier for tenants.
Reliance on personal information
10. The sharing of limited personal data relating to enforcement action is necessary for
the RLAC to achieve its stated aims. Wholly anonymised or pseudonymised data
would not support London boroughs in their efforts to crack down on landlords and
letting agents acting unlawfully, nor would it enable the public to check whether their
landlord or agent has faced enforcement action.
DESCRIPTION OF INFORMATION TO BE SHARED
Categories of information
11. The following categories of data will be shared with the public for landlord entries:
• landlord name (and ‘also known as’ or associated names, for instance
company names)
• home address of landlord – street name and first four digits of postcode only
• address of rental property(ies) associated with the offence
• name of enforcement authority
• criminal conviction offence
• date of conviction
• expiry date of conviction
• court where conviction took place
• value of fine
• conditional discharge offence
• date of conditional discharge
• expiry date of conditional discharge
• court where conditional discharge was awarded

• details of enforcement notices issued by London Fire Brigade
• details of prohibition notices issued by London Fire Brigade
• civil penalty notice offence
• date of civil penalty issue
12. The following categories of data will be shared with the public for letting agents:
• trading name
• ownership details (Ltd co, partnership, sole trader)
• names of principal officers
• registered office address
• other branches
• website
• membership of a trade body
• membership of a redress scheme
• address of rental property associated with the offence (where appropriate)
• name of enforcement authority
• criminal conviction offence
• date of conviction
• expiry date of conviction
• court where conviction took place
• value of fine
• conditional discharge offence
• date of conditional discharge
• expiry date of conditional discharge
• court where condition discharge was awarded
• trading standards civil penalty notice offence
• date of civil penalty issue
• details of expulsion from a redress scheme
13. As well as the information listed above, other enforcement activity taken against
landlords and letting agents will be shared on the non-public part of the RLAC,
including simple cautions.
14. A list of relevant offences can be found at Annex 2. This list may be subject to
change in consultation with all participating partners, for example when legislation
changes.
15. Records on the RLAC from the same landlord or letting agent will be linked to
demonstrate repeat offending. This link will be displayed on the public and non-
public part of the RLAC. Links between two or more records originating from a
single partner can be authorised by the partner themselves. Links from two or more
different participating partners will be suggested by the RLAC, based on records
containing the same (or very similar) names and home address of landlord, or
trading name and office address of letting agent. The participating partners will then
agree amongst themselves whether they are satisfied that this is the same person
or organisation and will then approve or deny the link based on the details of the
record. All partners associated with the entries will need to approve the link in order
for it go forward to publication.

16. Any data which relates to an identified or identifiable natural person, either on its
own or in conjunction with any other data held by either party, constitutes personal
data as defined under Article 4(1) of the UK General Data Protection Regulation
(UK GDPR),
17. For the purpose of this project, a company acting as a landlord or letting agent will
be treated in the same way as an individual.
18. Any data consisting of information as to the commission or alleged commission of
any offence by the data subject or any proceedings for any offence committed or
alleged to have been committed by the data subject, the disposal of such
proceedings or the sentence of any court in such proceedings, constitutes ‘data
relating to criminal convictions and offences’. The processing of personal data
about criminal convictions or offences must have a lawful basis under Article 6 of
UK GDPR and either legal authority or official authority for the processing under
Article 10 of UK GDPR
Source(s) of the information
19. The data will be sourced from databases or computer systems held by each London
borough and the London Fire Brigade.
Frequency
20. Uploads onto the RLAC will take place no longer than ten working days following
the enforcement outcome, subject to notification by the borough’s legal department.
21. Inaccurate personal data will be corrected no later than five working days after the
inaccuracy has been identified.
LEGAL BASIS FOR INFORMATION SHARING
Legal powers to share the information
22. London boroughs have a range of powers (contained in the Housing Acts and other
legislation) relating to the attainment of housing standards in the private rented
sector (including overcrowding, housing conditions, houses in multiple occupation
and the selective licensing of private landlords). Failures by private landlords to
attain minimum standards and compliance in these areas can be met by
enforcement action by the boroughs and lead to criminal sanctions. A list of relevant
offences can be found at Annex 2.
23. Local authorities also have a general power of competence, enabling them to do
anything that an individual may do (Localism Act 2011). They also have a power to
do anything which is calculated to facilitate, or is conducive or incidental to, the
discharge of any of their functions (Local Government Act 1972).
24. Section 17 Crime and Disorder Act 1998 requires the London boroughs to exercise
their various functions with due regard to the likely effect of the exercise of those
functions on, and the need to do all that it reasonably can to prevent
a) crime and disorder in their area (including anti-social and other behaviour
adversely affecting the local environment); and
b) the misuse of drugs, alcohol and other substances in their area; and
c) re-offending in their area.

25. The participation of the London boroughs in the RLAC is consistent with, and
incidental to, its various housing powers. Participation in a scheme to bring to the
public’s attention the action taken to tackle poor standards in the private rented
sector and the sanctions applied to landlords and agents acting unlawfully, will serve
to raise awareness, act as a deterrent and contribute to the improvement of housing
conditions in the private rented sector. It thus demonstrates that regard has been
paid to the need to prevent crime, disorder and behaviour adversely affecting the
local environment.
26. The RLAC initiative falls within the Mayor’s power in section 30 of the Greater
London Authority Act 1999 (the ‘GLA Act’) to act on behalf of the GLA to do anything
which he considers will further the promotion of economic and social development
in Greater London.
Inclusion of Housing and Planning Act Civil Penalties on the RLAC
27. The Housing and Planning Act (‘HPA’) 2016 introduced civil penalties of up to
£30,000 for certain specified housing offences under the Housing Act 2004 and
breaches of banning orders under the HPA. These civil penalties are designed to
be an alternative to prosecution.
28. Various boroughs have instructed the GLA to upload HPA civil penalties and trading
standards civil penalties of £500 or more to the public tier of the RLAC. These
boroughs are listed in the RLAC policies and procedures document which can be
found on the Rogue Landlord and Agent Checker website at:
www.london.gov.uk/rogue-landlord-checker. The boroughs in question will continue
to use their own discretion to decide which cases should be uploaded to the RLAC,
in line with their own policies and procedures.
General Data Protection Regulation
Statutory data processing conditions
29. Processing of personal data under this scheme must adhere to the ‘principles
relating to processing personal data’ under Article 5 of UK GDPR. The first of these
principles provides that personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly and in a
transparent manner.
30. The processing of personal data is only considered to be lawful if a legal basis under
Article 6(1) applies. The processing of data relating to criminal convictions and
offences must meet the requirement in Article 10 of UK GDPR.
Article 6 legal basis
31. The following legal basis under Article 6(1) applies to the processing of personal
data:
(e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public
interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller

32. Section 8(c) of the 2018 Act prescribes that Article 6(1)(e) of UK GDPR – processing
of personal data that is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the
public interest or in the exercise of the controller’s official authority – includes
processing of personal data that is necessary for the exercise of a function conferred
on a person by an enactment or rule of law. Section 8(c) of the 2018 Act has been
considered to apply by virtue of the legislation detailed in paragraphs 23 to 27 of
this procedure.
33. Processing of data is considered to be necessary to help ensure that the private
rented housing market operates in a fairer and more transparent way, and that
tenants are protected from exploitation by unscrupulous landlords and letting
agents.
34. Reliance on Article 6(1)(e) involves a test of necessity which requires a balance to
be struck between the objectives of the Checker and the impact on those whose
information is disclosed. Subject to the protections afforded by representations
procedure in Annex 1, and by committing not to publicise spent convictions, the
parties agree that processing will not amount to an unreasonable or unwarranted
interference.
Article 10 considerations
35. The processing of data relating to criminal convictions and offences meets the
requirement under Article 10 of UK GDPR by virtue of the conditions listed in parts
1, 2 or 3 of Schedule 1 of the 2018 Act. The processing of data relating to criminal
convictions and offences for the Checker will comply with paragraph 6(2)(a) of
Schedule 1 of the 2018 Act:
• processing.. is necessary for reasons of substantial public interest… and the
exercise of a function conferred on a person by an enactment or rule of law.
The legislation detailed in paragraphs 23 to 27 of this procedure describes the
applicable enactments.
36. The processing of data relating to criminal convictions and offences in this manner
is considered to be a matter of substantial public interest for the following reasons
a) London’s private rented sector is an important part of the London housing
market, and the number of private renters continues to rise. At the same time,
there is no easy way for tenants to find out about offences committed by a
prospective landlord or agent so that they can make an informed choice.
Addressing this information gap in relation to renting property in London is
deemed to be matter of public interest.
b) The processing allows London Boroughs and the London Fire Brigade to share
information in order to deliver more targeted enforcement activity and to assist
in cross-borough enforcement against rogue landlords and letting agents.
c) Given the importance of the private rented sector within London’s housing
market, the proposed processing contributes to meeting that interest, in a very
significant way, not in a marginal or trivial way. There is a rational connection
between the processing and the relevant substantial public interest.
d) The processing directly meets the information gap explained above in relation
to the London private rented sector.

Law enforcement processing
37. To the extent that the processing of personal data under this scheme is by a
competent authority for law enforcement purposes (as defined in section 31 of the
2018 Act), the processing meets the requirements of the six data protection
principles in section 34 of the 2018 Act. In relation to the requirements of section
35 of the 2018 Act, the processing is lawful since it is necessary for the performance
of a task carried out for law enforcement purposes by a competent authority.
Compatibility with original purpose for which personal data was captured
38. Data regarding enforcement action taken against landlords and agents is collected
as part of the Fire Brigade and London boroughs’ work to protect tenants and
improve conditions in the private rented sector. The RLAC is therefore compatible
with this purpose in that its principle objectives are to empower tenants to check
their landlord or agent’s history and deter unlawful practice in the sector.
Fair processing requirements
39. To support compliance with the first data processing principles under Article 5(1)(a)
of UK GDPR and meet the aforementioned conditions under schedules 2 and 3 of
the DPA requirements of Articles 13 and 14 of UK GDPR – the provision of ‘fair
processing’ information to data subjects - the GLA will write to the landlord or agent
on behalf of London Borough of Camden. prior to his/her data being displayed on
the RLAC. The GLA will send this letter within two working days of the entry being
added to the RLAC holding area.
40. This will notify the data subject (landlord or letting agent) about the data which will
be processed on behalf the borough or Fire Brigade and manner in which it will be
processed. The letter will detail the categories of data (from paragraphs 11 – 13)
which will be ultimately made accessible to the public, in addition to the data
processed on the non-public part of the RLAC.
41. This notification will also set out timescales for the processing of their information
and inform the data subject or agent of their right to make representations against
the processing of their personal data in the manner described and update any
inaccurate or incorrect data.
42. The representation process (see Annex 1) will cover the assessment of any
complaints made by the data subject and submissions concerning inaccurate or
incorrect data. This will ensure that the rights of the data subject are considered at
all times and balanced against the legitimate interests and legal obligations that
facilitate the processing of their personal data in this manner.
43. The GLA is required to keep clear and auditable records of the notification and
representation processes on behalf of London Borough of Camden. to ensure fair
processing of any personal data on behalf of the borough and demonstrate
compliance with the appropriate data processing conditions outlined above.
44. The above procedure does not override a data subject’s right at any time to exercise
their right to rectification (Article 16 of UK GDPR), to erasure (Article 17 of UK
GDPR), to restriction of processing (Article 18 of UK GDPR) or to object (Article 21

of UK GDPR). If the GLA is in receipt of any such notice they shall pass this to the
borough or Fire Brigade immediately upon receipt.
Data Controller status of the receiving party
45. London Borough of Camden is the controller of all the personal data covered by this
Procedure and is responsible for ensuring it is processed fairly and lawfully and in
accordance with the principles of UK GDPR and the 2018 Act.
46. On receipt of any personal data under this Procedure, the GLA shall become a
processor of that data and will only process that personal data in accordance with
this Information Sharing Procedure and the Data Processing Agreement at Annex
3 to this Procedure and for no other purposes. The GLA will at all times ensure that
any of its staff with access to the RLAC are trained in the applicable data protection
law in force at that time. The GLA will ensure that appropriate technical and
organisational measures shall be taken against unauthorised or unlawful processing
of personal data and against accidental loss or destruction of or damage to personal
or sensitive personal data on the RLAC when exercising its functions as data
processor.
47. Although the GLA is registered as a controller in its own right for projects outside of
the scope of this agreement, the London Borough of Camden shall remain the
controller in respect of the data for this project and continue to process the data for
their own purposes.
Human Rights Act 1998
48. Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights prescribes that everyone
has a right to respect for private and family life, home and correspondence. There
shall be no interference by a public authority with the exercise of this right except
such as in accordance with the law and is necessary in a democratic society in the
interests of national security, public safety or the economic wellbeing of the country,
for the prevention of disorder or crime, for the protection of health or morals or for
the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.
49. The sharing of personal data under this Procedure will comply with Article 8 as it is:
In pursuit of a legitimate aim: The Mayor of London has a general power to do
anything that will promote economic and social development, and environmental
improvement in London. The Rogue landlord and agent Checker would be one way
of achieving this by seeking to ensure the private rented housing market operates
in a fairer and more transparent way, and that tenants are protected from
exploitation by unscrupulous landlords and letting agents.
Proportionate: In each case, the information shared will be proportionate for the
purpose - the minimum information necessary for the purpose will be transferred
and no more.
Appropriate and necessary to a democratic society: Promoting social development,
making London’s communities a better place to live, and protecting tenants from
criminal landlords and letting agents are all activities appropriate to a democratic
society. Each individual will have the opportunity to correct any inaccuracy prior to

publication and to explain whether there is a reason why it would not be in the public
interest to disclose their convictions (see Annex 1).
Common law Duty of Confidentiality
50. In this case, there are no circumstances that would create an expectation of
confidentiality on the part of any individuals whose data is shared between the
partners for the agreed purposes.
Crime and Disorder Act 1998
51. Section 17 of the Crime and Disorder Act 1998 has been considered and without
prejudice to any other obligation imposed on it, the RLAC is compatible with its
various functions with due regard to the likely effect of exercising those functions to
do all that it reasonably can to prevent, crime and disorder in its area (including anti-
social and other behaviour adversely affecting the local environment).
52. The use of the RLAC will provide valuable intelligence to boroughs in regulating and
investigating landlords operating across borough boundaries. Landlords acting
unlawfully are directly linked to high levels of anti-social behaviour, poor property
management and enviro-crimes in the private rented sector; the RLAC will protect
tenants and prospective tenants from these landlords thus preventing further crime
and disorder.
MEANS OF TRANSFER
53. Information subject to this sharing agreement will be shared via the RLAC whereby
both London Borough of Camden and the GLA will have mutual access to
information once entered on to the system, subject to the appropriate access
controls.
54. London Borough of Camden will input existing convictions into an excel spreadsheet
containing mandatory fields and optional fields. This document will then be
uploaded as a CSV file onto the RLAC. Following upload onto the RLAC, the entries
will remain in the holding area whilst the GLA notifies the data subject (see Annex
1 for details on the holding area and notification). Subsequent entries will be added
using a manual entry online form.
INFORMATION HANDLING ARRANGMENTS
Access to the information shared
55. RLAC users on the non-public tier will have one of three types of access: super-
users, viewing and editing, or viewing-only.
a) Super-users will be able to view all published information held on each entry, upload
new entries, edit existing entries, make decisions regarding representations, and
manage the viewing-only and viewing and editing users for their borough. There
will be two super-users per borough.

b) Viewing and editing users will be able to log into the RLAC and view all published
information held on each entry. They will be able to add new entries to the RLAC
and edit existing entries.
c) Viewing-only users will be able to log into the RLAC and view all published
information held on each entry. This user type will be given to most enforcement
officers at each borough.
56. At the GLA, the following roles will have super-user access:
• Private Rented Sector Manager, Housing and Land Directorate
• Senior Projects Officer, Housing and Land Directorate
• Project Officer, Housing and Land Directorate
57. At London Borough of Camden the following officers (job roles) will require super-
user access:
1. Operations Manager
2. Principal EHO
58. User accounts for London Borough of Camden employees will be created, deleted
and managed by the super-user. Each user will have one account, and there will
be strictly no sharing of log ins. User accounts of all type must be kept up to date,
for example when officers resign the account should be deleted promptly.
Regular or routine onward disclosures by the recipient of the Information
59. The GLA is in ongoing discussions with the Ministry of Housing, Communities and
Local Government (MHCLG) about the development of a national criminal landlord
database as provided for by the Housing and Planning Act 2016. The MHCLG
database will request from boroughs a very similar set of data to that which is to be
included in the RLAC.
60. If agreement is secured from MHCLG, London Borough of Camden in principle
authorises the GLA to act as an intermediary between the two systems, effectively
passing on the data from the London RLAC to the national database, on behalf of
the data controller (London Borough of Camden). The GLA will pass on any data
which is eligible under the conditions for the database set out in the Housing and
Planning Act 2016. Further engagement with all boroughs concerned would need
to be undertaken and written agreement obtained prior to any data being
transferred.
Unplanned or ad hoc onward disclosures of the Information
61. There will be no unplanned disclosure without prior consent from the relevant data
controller. Each participating partner will only be required to notify the original
source of the Information where a secondary disclosure has been made to a third
party for a purpose unconnected to those provided for in this Information Sharing
Procedure.
Retention period(s)
62. Personal data will only be retained for the minimum length of time necessary to
achieve the specific aims of each function of the RLAC.

First tier public view
a) Civil penalties of £500 or more will remain publicly viewable for up to 12 months
after the enforcement action date. Prior to 1 July 2021, the enforcement action
date was taken as the date that the final civil penalty notice was served.
Information as to how the enforcement action date is currently calculated may be
found by referring to the RLAC policies and procedures document which can be
found on the Rogue Landlord and Agent Checker website at:
www.london.gov.uk/rogue-landlord-checker.
b) Criminal convictions will remain accessible to the public until the date at which the
conviction becomes spent. The vast majority of landlord and letting agent
convictions will result in a fine, meaning that the conviction becomes spent after
12 months.
c) In rare cases, a prosecution may result in a custodial sentence, meaning the
conviction will last longer than 12 months in line with the provisions of the Legal
Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. Each new entry on the
RLAC will be set to a default removal data of 12 months following the date of
conviction. It will be possible for the data controller to override this in instances of
a custodial sentence.
d) Enforcement and prohibition notices from the London Fire Brigade will have a
retention period of three years, mirroring the retention policy for the London Fire
Brigade website.
e) The London Borough of Camden can reduce the retention period for their entries if
this is considered appropriate.
Second tier borough view
f) Entries will remain accessible to the user groups specified in section 53 for a
maximum of ten years, at which point they will be automatically deleted from the
RLAC. This retention period is considered to be appropriate because it reflects
the period over which the information remains relevant and useful to the boroughs
for enforcement purposes. The RLAC will default the deletion date to ten years,
but London Borough of Camden is able to override this if they feel a shorter
retention period is appropriate. In no circumstances can the retention period
exceed the maximum set out in this procedure for each entry.
Secure storage and destruction of the information
63. Further to the arrangements discussed elsewhere in this Information Sharing
Procedure, and those specified in the Data Processing Agreement at Annex 3, the
user guide developed to cover use of the shared RLAC will detail specific access
controls, process or other security measure that will be implemented to protect the
information held on the RLAC and the integrity of those systems in accordance with
the guidance issued by the Information Commissioner’s Office.

64. From April 2022 data will not be transferred outside the United Kingdom at any
point and the RLAC will reside on servers based in the UK.
65. Access to the non-public RLAC will be restricted by IP address. Only IP address
from participating partners (the GLA, London Fire Brigade and London boroughs)
will be given access.
66. All data will be encrypted in transit. In the instance where a spreadsheet is used
for transfer, only a CSV file will be used.
67. The RLAC will be penetration tested annually by the GLA, and results will be
shared with London Borough of Camden.
68. All passwords for the RLAC will comply with NCSC latest guidelines.
69. All user access and actions on the RLAC will be logged for auditing purposes.
Notification and rectification of errors in the information
70. Information shared by London Borough of Camden is subject to procedures and
validations to ensure data quality at the point it is originally input/entered into its own
systems.
71. On becoming aware of incorrect data, London Borough of Camden will correct their
record on the RLAC as soon as possible and no later than five working days after
becoming aware of that error.
72. The GLA, as the receiving authority and data processor, will not amend any
personal data provided by London Borough of Camden without explicit instruction
from London Borough of Camden, as the data controller.
73. Annex 1 to this procedure details the representations process whereby a landlord
or agent can request rectification of any inaccuracies.
Freedom of Information Act 2000 (FoIA)
74. The GLA and London Borough of Camden are both public authorities for the
purposes of the FoIA. This means that any information held by either the GLA or
London Borough of Camden, is accessible by the public on written request, subject
to certain limited exemptions.
75. The GLA and London Borough of Camden shall demonstrate a commitment to
openness and transparency regarding information sharing arrangements in
accordance with this Procedure and the Overarching Information Sharing Protocol
between both parties, subject to any limitations posed by security or confidentiality
requirements.
76. In the event that an information access request is received relating to information
covered by this procedure, both parties agree to notify one another within five
working days following receipt of the request and to consult with the other in line
with the Code of Practice as implemented by section 45 of FoIA.

77. For the purposes of information held on the shared RLAC, information is only
considered to be “held for the purposes of FoIA” by the party which was responsible
for the original record being entered on to the shared RLAC – read-only access to
information entered on to the RLAC by another public authority does not constitute
“information held” for the purposes of FoIA.
78. Any non-public personal data considered in response to a Freedom of Information
Act request should be anonymised and withheld as appropriate under section 40(2)
of that Act.
Subject Access Requests (SAR)
79. Article 15 of UK GDPR provides the right to an individual to request access to
personal data of which they are the subject. This also includes the right to be told
the purposes for which their data is being processed and with whom their data is
being shared.
80. London Borough of Camden is the controller of all the personal data covered by this
Procedure and is responsible for handling and considering any Subject Access
Request made in relation to any personal data for which they are the controller.
81. If the GLA receives a Subject Access Request that relates solely to the personal
data held on the RLAC, the GLA will notify the London Borough of Camden (as the
controller of that personal data) within five working days of receiving such a request,
and promptly notify the data subject making the request that they should re-submit
their SAR to London Borough of Camden.
82. On receipt of any SAR, London Borough of Camden will notify the GLA within five
working days if the request covers any data or information processed by the GLA
on behalf of London Borough of Camden which cannot otherwise be accessed via
the RLAC.
83. The GLA must provide London Borough of Camden with any information or data
which is subject to a SAR that is processed on behalf of London Borough of Camden
as soon as possible and no later than ten working days following receipt of an
instruction from the London Borough of Camden.
84. The GLA will remain a controller in its own right for other information and personal
data processed outside of this agreement and the Data Processing Agreement at
Annex 3. This agreement will not affect the ability of the GLA to process its own
personal data for its own purposes in line with the GLA’s obligations and legal
requirements.
85. The obligation for the GLA (as a processor) to provide London Borough of Camden
(as a controller) with personal data processed on their behalf under the terms of this
agreement does not extend to include any other personal data that might be held
by the GLA for its own purposes outside of this agreement in a situation where the
GLA also holds different personal data on the same data subject for a different
purpose.
OTHER CONDITIONS

86. This Procedure does not require adherence to any other conditions. London
Borough of Camden and the GLA agree to inform the other party should any new
applicable conditions arise.
LOSS OR UNAUTHORISED DISCLOSURES OF INFORMATION
87. Examples of loss or unauthorised disclosure may include:
• Accidental loss or damage to Information
• Damage or loss of information by means of malicious software/hacking
• Deliberate or knowing disclosure of Information to a person not entitled to
receive it.
88. The designated points of contact are responsible for notifying the other party in the
event of loss or unauthorised disclosures of data within 24 hours of the event.
89. The designated points of contact will also be responsible for notifying other relevant
internal departments. In the case of London Borough of Camden, this will be the Data
Protection Officer at DPO@camden.gov.uk and the GLA it will be the GLA Data
Protection Officer.
REVIEW
90. This Information Sharing Procedure will be reviewed as and when changes to

VARIATION
92. This procedure shall only be varied or amended with the written agreement of both

Annex 1: Landlord and agent representations process
1. Overview
1.1. As set out above, London boroughs and the London Fire Brigade will submit
information to the RLAC on landlords and letting agents. The GLA will offer
landlords and letting agents whose details have been submitted the opportunity to
make representations prior to them being displayed. It is envisioned this process
will work as follows.
2. Data ‘holding area’
2.1. London boroughs and the fire brigade will upload records onto the RLAC within
ten days of the enforcement outcome. This record will then sit in a ‘holding area’
on the RLAC – the ‘holding area’ is a non-public section of the Checker which
allows the record to remain unpublished whilst the GLA prints and distributes a
letter to the landlord/agent notifying them of their inclusion on the RLAC. While in
the holding area, the record will only be available to view by the GLA and the
partner which has uploaded it.
3. Representations
3.1. Representations from landlords or letting agents will be received in response to
the notification letter preceding publication or once the entry has already been
published. Landlords and letting agents could make post-publication
representations multiple times if their circumstances change and the following
applies to representations on both unpublished and published entries.
Representations made after publication will remain on the RLAC while a decision
is being reached.
3.2. All representation information, notes and documents will be visible to the GLA and
the uploading participating partner only. The public and other participating
partners will not be able to see any of this information.
3.3. Within two working days of the entry being added, notification letters will be sent to
landlords and letting agents giving them 14 days to make representations prior to
publication. Landlords or lettings agents who believe their data should not be
displayed will need to include full supporting evidence. For instance, if a landlord
claims a conviction has been overturned, they must provide evidence that this is
the case. During this 14-day period the entry will remain in the holding area and
not be displayed.
3.4. Representations from the landlord or letting agents will generally be received by
the GLA via email. The GLA will act as an intermediary and pass on such
representations to the relevant controller (London borough or London Fire
Brigade) for consideration and decision via the RLAC.
4. Decisions
4.1. From receipt of the representation, the controller has ten working days to
investigate the representation. The controller will remain responsible for the
decision to remove records from the checker, amend the record or proceed to

publication. Once the controller has reached a decision, they will log this decision
against the entry on the RLAC. The GLA will receive notification of this decision.
The GLA will then send out a letter notifying the landlord or agent of the decision
reached. Following distribution of this letter, the landlord or agent has a further 14
days within which to take further action.
4.2. The record will only go on to be displayed on the public RLAC:
• once 14 days have elapsed following the letter of notification and no
representation has been received, or
• once a representation has been received, a decision has been reached by
the controller to reject the representation and proceed to publication, and
14 days have elapsed following the GLA notifying the data subject of the
outcome of their representation.
4.3. Decisions regarding representations are to be made solely by the controller.
Decisions must meet one of the following exceptional criteria:
• Naming on the RLAC carries a risk of personal harm to an individual or
their family
• There are national security risks associated with naming an individual
• There are other factors which suggest that it would not be in the public
interest to name an individual.
