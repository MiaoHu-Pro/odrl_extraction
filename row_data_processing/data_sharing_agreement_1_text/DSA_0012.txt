1 Introduction
This protocol is an agreement between the following parties to share personal
information held on police and local authority databases on a regular basis.
This document is not a legally binding document. It aims to provide the basis for an
agreement between the agencies and other organisations engaged in the Rescue
and Response project to facilitate and govern the efficient, effective and secure
sharing of good quality information. It sets out:
• The principles underpinning information sharing
• The general purposes for information sharing
• The responsibilities and commitments of partners to this agreement
• The arrangements for monitoring and review
This protocol aligns with all other protocols to which agencies may already be
signatories and does not in any way supersede those existing agreements.
It is not intended that this document be definitive or exhaustive, it is recognised that
as policy develops and implementation arrangements mature, this protocol will need
to be reviewed and amended in light of new information sharing requirements to
ensure that it is ‘fit for purpose’.
As this protocol aims to provide a set of guiding principles for information sharing
within the context of the Rescue and Response project to which partners can sign
up, its purpose is not to be specific. It is a key principle of this agreement that this
protocol should be underpinned by a series of specific data sharing agreements that
detail the specific data/information sharing requirements between agencies.
2 Policy context
The Rescue and Response project will develop and deliver a support service for
young people who are vulnerable and caught up in county lines drug distribution
networks across London. This issue brings together gangs, national policing
strategy, CSE, trafficking, exploitation and vulnerability; underpinned by a complex
range of social and structural disadvantage. The need has been identified but is so
far only partly understood and sporadically mapped, with support models untested at
any scale.
This project will be the first county lines support service that operates at scale - pan
London and for three years. The project includes both support providers and
intelligence analysis working together. It is led by a coalition of London boroughs
(Brent, Islington, Tower Hamlets and Lewisham) with support from Metropolitan

Police Service special commands and the all London boroughs and highly
experienced London based gangs’ agencies.
In order for the development of the Rescue and Response project to be successful, it
is essential that all agencies and organisations engaged in its development and
implementation are empowered and committed to share good quality and relevant
information in a responsible and secure way.
This agreement deals with the need to share personal information in order to
identify young people from London who are being exploited through County
Lines, to then monitor them, support them by referring to voluntary agencies,
and possibly conduct enforcement action against those linked to County Lines.
This work will take place as part of the Rescue and Response project. It seeks to
fulfil the aims and ambitions outlined in the MOPAC Police and Crime Plan for
multiple agencies to: work together to keep children and young people safe,
prevent violence against women and girls, reduce reoffending and repeat
victimisation as well as enabling the Community Safety Partnership to meet its
Statutory Obligations. It is intended to focus partnership activity on identifying
and supporting young people from London who are being exploited through
county lines, as well as targeting those offenders who are linked to county lines
exploitation.
Information will be shared in order to make informed decisions about the young
people referred to the Rescue and Response project and which agency will provide
direct support to those young people. Partner agencies will pool their information
together regarding the young people identified as being exploited. The list of
young people identified and referred will be: shared with the London borough they
reside in, or come from; identified for support either through the Rescue and
Response project, or alternative borough based interventions; an assessment
made as to their level of engagement with partners and the most appropriate
agency provide support intervention. This exchange of information will continue
as the young people are supported by the partners of this multi-agency scheme.
This will be an ongoing process as young people’s risk increases, move
geographically, and where relevant, come out of prison. This will mean that there
will always be young people who are being considered for the project, move within
the project and face removal from the project.
Information shared through the course of a young person being identified and
referred to the Rescue and Response project will allow the partnership to:
(a) Gain a complete picture of a young person’s support needs, risks, and their
engagement with support services, in order to inform choices about further
support and or enforcement activity.
(b) Increase in number of young people identified as being exploited across
London

(g) Increased feelings of safety and reduced feelings of vulnerability
(h) Monitor their ongoing progress and engagement with support services and

(i) Ensure that partners have the most up to date information when dealing

(j) Ensure that partners have the most up to date information when risk

The aims and objectives of the Rescue and Response Project is to provide
effective support to young people from London being exploited through County
Lines programme, and identify young people earlier, in order to:
 Reduce crime
 Safeguard young people earlier
 Reduction in recorded criminal behaviour of the young people referred
 Increase in number of Young People identified as being exploited
 Increased engagement with services
 Better understanding of the problem and networks
 Increase in number of Young people identified as being at risk of county
lines exploitation
 Reduce reoffending,
 Make safer communities,
 Improve public confidence in the criminal justice system,
The information sharing under this agreement will allow partners to provide
the best range of services to the young people whilst addressing any
continuing offending or safeguarding concerns.

3 Scope
As stated above the protocol covers the sharing of information between all agencies
and organisations engaged in/or who are identified as holding relevant information
for the purposes of developing, implementing, monitoring and evaluating the Rescue
and Response project.
A list of signatories is included at Annex A. This is not exhaustive and will be
updated regularly as part of the protocol monitoring and review process as required.
The lawful basis for sharing is established in the relevant, specific ISA for the
purpose, which sits beneath this protocol (see Section 6 of ISA)
Information will be shared in order to make informed decisions about the young people
referred to the Rescue and Response project and which agency will provide direct
support to those young people.
Referrals will be made to the Rescue & Response Project Team by agencies across
the country, where they concern young people living in London.
Partner agencies will pool their information together regarding the young people
identified as being exploited. The list of young people identified and referred will be:
shared with the London borough they reside in, or come from; identified for support
either through the Rescue and Response project, or alternative borough based
interventions; an assessment made as to their level of engagement with partners and
the most appropriate agency provide support intervention. This exchange of
information will continue as the young people are supported by the partners of this
multi-agency scheme. This will be an ongoing process as young people’s risk
increases, move geographically, and where relevant, come out of prison. This will
mean that there will always be young people who are being considered for the project,
move within the project and face removal from the project.
Information may be personal or sensitive in nature and may be shared where there is
a legal power to do so.
The relevance of the scope of the protocol should be considered as part of a regular
monitoring and review process.
4 Purposes for sharing information
Under the terms of this agreement information may be shared for any purpose that
supports the development of the Rescue and Response project and that has been
agreed within a specific information sharing agreement. In general, information
sharing will be required to support the development and operation of the Rescue and
Response project.

6 Legislative context
Version 1 - October 2018 Page 9 of 18 www.brent.gov.uk

It is essential that all information shared under the terms of this protocol will be done
so in compliance with the legislation listed in Section 6 of the ISA.
All aspects of these acts are relevant to the context under this protocol.
In addition, each provider should have an internal operational policy to ensure
implementation of appropriate safeguarding processes. These should incorporate the
requirement to immediately bring to the attention of the safeguarding and commissioning
teams any allegation, complaint or suspicion of abuse by or regarding any service user.
This is whether the suspected abuser is employed by the provider, by the council or by
any other person.
In addition each agency / organisation signed up to this protocol will have their own
legal framework that governs their functions and that sets out the circumstances
under which personal and sensitive information may be shared.
It is the responsibility of the individual agency/organisation to ensure that their data
sharing transactions undertaken are done so legally and fairly and that they comply
with their own legal powers and the legislation detailed above.
All Parties to this agreement shall, from 25 May 2018, comply with its obligations
under the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of
natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free
movement of such data (the "GDPR"). For the purpose of this clause, a reference in
this agreement to the DPA shall from 25 May 2018 be construed as being a
reference to the GDPR and any defined terms given the meanings that are ascribed
to them in the DPA shall have the meaning given to them in the GDPR. Any
reference in this agreement to "sensitive personal data" shall from 25 May 2018 be
construed as meaning "special categories of personal data" as referred to in the
GDPR.

7 Key principles:
The Partner Organisations recognise that many services cannot be effectively
delivered without the exchange of Personal Information across key public sector,
private and voluntary organisations.
In signing up to this Protocol the signatories agree and commit to the following
principles:
1. This protocol aims to align with individual partner agency statutory, legal and
common law duties.
2. This agreement is to be entered into alongside any existing protocols,
procedures, policies and guidance to which the partner already adheres and
does not supersede them.
3. This protocol will be underpinned by individual data sharing agreements that
will govern data sharing transactions between partners.
4. Information will only be used for the purposes stated in this protocol, and as
detailed in individual information sharing agreements.
5. Partner agencies comply with the requirements of the Data Protection Act
2018 and in particular the data protection principles.
6. Partner agencies support, endorse and promote the accurate, timely, secure
and confidential sharing of information for the purposes stated in this protocol.
7. Where it is agreed that it is necessary to share personal information, it will be
shared only on a ‘need to know’ basis and this will be detailed in the individual
information sharing agreements. All other information will be statistical and
aggregated.
8. Personal and sensitive information will only be shared under this agreement
where there is a statutory power to do so and the conditions for processing as
determined in the Data Protection Act 2018 can be met.
9. Agencies agree to ensure that data sharing takes place in accordance with
their legal, statutory and common law duties and that responsibility for
ensuring that they have adequate notifications, privacy notices, retention
schedules, policies, procedures and guidance to do so remains with them.
10. Signatories agree to the roles and responsibilities set out in section 9
11. All information shared shall be classified according to the Government
Security Classifications Policy (GSCP). The handling and access of the data
shall comply with the requirements of this classification.
12. In the event of a Security Incident, the responsible Partner Organisation
should immediately inform the Governing Group and all other affected Partner
Organisations (usually the disclosing Partner Organisation(s)) with as many
details as known at that time and regularly update the relevant Partner
Organisations and Governing Group thereafter, including any subsequent
investigation report or remedial actions. Any affected Partner Organisation will
Version 1 - October 2018 Page 11 of 18 www.brent.gov.uk

then pass on the information in accordance with incident reporting procedures
within their own organisation if appropriate.
13. Partner Organisations will follow the decision tree at Annex C.
14. Information shared pursuant to this Protocol may not be shared with any other
organisation not a signatory to this Protocol without prior consent of the
relevant Partner Organisation and/or data subject.
15. Each Partner Organisation shall comply with individual rights as defined under
the Data Protection Act 2018, Freedom of Information Act 2005 and the
Human Rights Act 1998.
16. All information will be supplied in line with the relevant standards for
information quality and security. The systems and technical measures used
by each Partner Organisation for the sharing of personal information shall be
specified in any information sharing agreement.
17. Each Partner Organisation shall minimise the use of paper in sharing and
storing information. Information in transit shall be secure and encrypted. Use
of removable storage devices shall be prohibited. Paper in transit shall only be
undertaken if adequate safeguard are in place and the information is
minimised.
18. Use of public WI-FI shall be prohibited.
19. If a Partner Organisation is aware of a material inaccuracy or omission in
information that it shares under an Information Sharing Agreement, the
Partner Organisation must inform the recipient of that inaccuracy or omission.
20. Each Partner Organisation will nominate a person as a key contact to deal
with queries and requests for information under this Protocol.
21. Each Partner Organisation is responsible for putting into place effective
procedures to address complaints about data sharing and subject access
requests relating directly to this Protocol. Information about these procedures
should be made available to data subjects. Any complaints about data sharing
relating directly to this Protocol should be directed promptly to the Data
Protection Officer or Information Governance Manager of the relevant Partner
Organisation.

9 Supporting policies:
It is important that partners commit to ensuring that all information sharing is in line
with their own existing standards, policy and guidance with reference to:
• Information quality
• Information security
• Data protection
• Confidentiality
• Employee/Staff induction and training
Individual agencies are responsible for detailing their requirements for compliance in
their individual information sharing agreements and are responsible for their own
processes and procedures to monitor and ensure compliance.
10 Governance, monitoring and review
The review, monitoring and amendment of the protocol will be undertaken by the
Governing Group sitting under the Strategic Group. Formal review will be undertaken
annually unless legislation or policy changes dictate otherwise.
New parties to this agreement may be included at any time, the formal arrangements
for which will be managed by lead boroughs and/or as agreed by the Strategic Group
or Lead Borough’s Heads of Community Protection.
All amendments to the protocol will be reported to and signed off by the Governing
Group sitting under the Strategic Group.
11 Conclusion
This protocol proposes a consistent approach to the sharing of information between
partner agencies for the purposes of developing and implementing the Rescue and
Response project.
All partners need to be able to balance the conflicting demands between the need to
share information with other agencies and the requirement to maintain
confidentiality. These conflicting demands are acknowledged by this protocol which
provides a basis for partners to be confident that where information is shared it will

be done in a consistent, responsible and secure way for the purpose of safeguarding
young people involved with the Rescue and Response project.
