BACKGROUND
A. The following agreement between the Parties reflects the arrangements that they
have agreed to put in place to facilitate the joint working between the Psychologically
Informed Consultation and Training (PICT) and the Council’s Landlord Services
B. The Parties are data controllers and this Agreement explains the purposes for which
the Personal Data may be used.
C. As such, the Parties agree to share the Personal Data with each other on the terms set
out in this Agreement.

2. PURPOSE
2.1 This Agreement sets out the framework for the sharing of Personal Data between the
Parties as Data Controllers and defines the principles and procedures that the
Parties shall adhere to and the responsibilities the Parties owe to each other.
2.2 The Personal Data subject to this Agreement is listed within the Schedule.
2.3 The sharing of Personal Data is necessary to support the Agreed Purposes as listed
within the Schedule.
2.4 It is recognised that there are significant benefits to the Parties of having access to
the Shared Personal Data to achieve the Agreed Purposes.
4

2.5 This Agreement is required to ensure that where Personal Data may be accessed,
such access will at all times comply with the Data Protection Legislation.
3. COMPLIANCE
3.1 The Parties agree that this Agreement formalises the lawful transfer of the Personal
Data between the Parties and presents no new or additional privacy concerns.
3.2 A data protection impact assessment has been conducted in respect of the Personal
Data to be shared and the necessity of the sharing and this Agreement serves to
address any residual privacy or information risks and documents the actions taken to
identify, address and mitigate those risks wherever possible.
3.3 The Parties shall not process the Personal Data in a way that is incompatible with the
Agreed Purposes.
3.4 As Data Controllers, the Parties remain responsible for ensuring that all uses of the
Personal Data are in compliance with all applicable Data Protection Legislation.
3.5 This Agreement must be formally approved and signed by the Parties before any
Personal Data is shared by persons with authority to do so on behalf of their
organisation. All Parties will ensure that the terms of this Agreement are known and
understood by all Staff involved in the sharing of the Personal Data.
3.6 Each Party is registered with the ICO has a valid Data Protection Registration
Number with its national Data Protection Authority if required by the Enterprise Act
2017 which by the time that the data sharing is expected to commence, covers the
intended data sharing pursuant to this Agreement.
3.7 The Council’s ICO registration number is Z5948361 and the Provider’s ICO
registration number is Z8583439.
4. PERSONAL DATA
4.1 The Parties shall, in relation to any of the Personal Data processed in connection with
its obligations under this Agreement:
4.1.1 Process that Personal Data only in accordance with the Schedule, unless the
Parties are required to do otherwise by Law, which should be notified to the
other Parties (unless notification is prohibited by Law);
4.1.2 not Process in an irrelevant or excessive way with regard to the Agreed
Purposes;
4.1.3 ensure they do not knowingly or negligently do or omit to do anything which
places the Parties breach of the obligations under the Data Protection
Legislation;
4.1.4 ensure that they have in place Protective Measures to protect against:
a) unauthorised or unlawful processing of Personal Data;
b) accidental loss or destruction of, or damage to, Personal Data,
and such measures to be reviewed as appropriate to protect against a Data
Loss Event having taken account of the:
(i) nature of the data to be protected;
(ii) harm that might result from a Data Loss Event;
(iii) state of technological development; and
(iv) cost of implementing any measures;
4.1.5 ensure the Personal Data is kept and stored securely in accordance with Data
Protection Legislation;
4.1.6 ensure that:
(i) the Staff do not process Personal Data except in accordance the
Schedule;
(ii) each Party takes all reasonable steps to ensure the reliability and

integrity of any Staff who have access to the Personal Data and ensure
that they:
(A) are aware of and comply with the duties under this Agreement;
(B) are subject to appropriate confidentiality undertakings;
(C) are informed of the confidential nature of the Personal Data and do
not publish, disclose or divulge any of the Personal Data to any third
party unless permitted by this Agreement; and
(D) have undergone adequate training in the use, care, protection and
handling of the Personal Data;
4.1.7 not transfer the Personal Data outside of the UK unless the prior written

c) the Parties outside the UK comply with the obligations under the Data
Protection Legislation by providing an adequate level of protection to any

endeavours to assist the Parties in meeting its obligations under the

required by Law to retain the Personal Data; and
4.1.9 as far as is reasonably practical, anonymise or pseudoanonymise all Personal
Data contained in statistics, reports and summaries to minimise the amount of
Personal Data shared.
5. FAIR, LAWFUL AND TRANSPARENT PROCESSING
5.1 The Personal Data shall be shared in accordance with the Agreed Purposes on the
basis of the legal grounds as stated in the Schedule.
5.2 The Parties undertake to inform Data Subjects of the purposes for which it will
process their Personal Data and provide all of the information that it must provide in
accordance with applicable laws, to ensure that the Data Subjects understand how
their Personal Data will be processed by the Data Controller.
5.3 The Parties shall, in respect of the Personal Data, ensure that their privacy notices
are clear and provide sufficient information to Data Subjects, in order for them to
understand:
5.3.1 what Personal Data the Parties are sharing;
5.3.2 the circumstances in which it will be shared;
5.3.3 the purposes for the sharing;
5.3.4 either the identity with whom the Personal Data is shared or a description of
the type of organisation that will receive the Personal Data
unless an exemption to the transparency provisions applies.
6. DATA QUALITY
6.1 The Data Discloser shall ensure that the Personal Data is accurate.
6.2 Where either Party becomes aware of inaccuracies in the Personal Data, they will
notify the other Party immediately.
7. DATA SUBJECTS' RIGHTS
7.1 Data Subjects have the right to obtain certain information about the processing of
their Personal Data through a Subject Access Request. Data Subjects may also
request rectification, erasure or blocking of their Personal Data.
6

7.2 The Parties shall maintain a record of Subject Access Requests, the decisions made
and any information that was exchanged. Records must include copies of the request
for information, details of the data accessed and shared and where relevant, notes of
any meeting, correspondence or phone calls relating to the request, together with
relevant dates.
7.3 The Parties agree that the responsibility for complying with a Subject Access Request
falls to Party receiving the Subject Access Request in respect of the Personal Data
held by that Party.
7.4 The Parties agree to provide reasonable and prompt assistance (within 5 business
days of such a request for assistance) at their own expense as is necessary to each
other to enable them to comply with Subject Access Requests and to respond to any
other queries or complaints from Data Subjects.
8. DATA RETENTION AND DELETION
8.1 The Data Receiver shall not retain or process Personal Data for longer than is
necessary to carry out the Agreed Purposes.
8.2 Notwithstanding Clause 8.1, the Parties shall continue to retain the Personal Data in
accordance with any internal or statutory retention periods applicable in their
organisations and/or industry.
8.3 The Data Receiver shall ensure that any Personal Data is returned to the Data
Discloser or destroyed in the following circumstances:
(a) upon termination of the Agreement for whatever reason;
(b) upon expiry of the Term (unless extended further in accordance the terms of this
Agreement); and
(c) once processing of the Personal Data is no longer necessary for the Agreed
Purposes.
9. TRANSFERS
9.1 For the purposes of this Clause, transfers of Personal Data shall mean any sharing of
the Personal Data by the Data Receiver with a third party, and shall include, but is not
limited to, the following:
a) sharing of the Personal Data with any third party;
b) publication of the Personal Data via any medium, including, but not limited to
social media, websites, publically available communications.
c) storing Personal Data on servers outside the UK;
d) subcontracting the processing of Personal Data to data processors located
outside the UK;
e) granting third parties located outside the UK access rights to the Personal Data.
9.2 The Data Receiver shall not share the Personal Data with a third party without the
express written permission of the Data Discloser save as required by law or court
order.
9.3 Where Personal Data is to be shared under clause 9.2, the Data Receiver shall not
disclose or transfer the Personal Data outside the UK without first ensuring that
adequate and equivalent protections will be afforded to the Personal Data.
10. SECURITY AND TRAINING
10.1 The Parties agree to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to
protect the Personal Data in their possession against unauthorised or unlawful
processing and against accidental loss, destruction, damage, alteration or disclosure,
including but not limited to:
7

a) ensuring IT equipment, including portable equipment is kept in lockable areas
when unattended;
b) not leaving portable equipment containing the Personal Data unattended;
c) ensuring Staff use appropriate secure passwords for logging into systems or
databases containing the Personal Data;
d) ensuring that all IT equipment is protected by antivirus software, firewalls,
passwords and suitable encryption devices;
e) ensuring that any Special Category Personal Data is stored and transferred
(including where stored or transferred on portable devices or removable media)
using industry standard 256-bit AES encryption or suitable equivalent;
f) limiting access to relevant databases and systems to those Staff who need to
have access to the Personal Data, and ensuring that passwords are changed
and updated regularly to prevent inappropriate access when individuals are no
longer engaged by the Party;
g) conducting regular threat assessment or penetration testing on systems;
h) ensuring all Staff handling Personal Data have been made aware of their
responsibilities with regards to handling of the Personal Data; and
i) allowing for inspections and assessments to be undertaken by the other Party in
respect of the security measures taken, or producing evidence of those
measures if requested.
11. PERSONAL DATA BREACHES AND REPORTING PROCEDURES
11.1 The Parties shall ensure they do not knowingly or negligently do or omit to do anything
which places either Party in breach of the Data Protection Legislation.
11.2 Each Party shall inform the other immediately if it:
a) is in breach of the Protective Measures requirements;
b) receives a request to rectify, block or erase the Personal Data;
c) receives any complaint or communication relating to either Party's obligations
under the Data Protection Legislation;
d) receives any communication from the Information Commissioner or any other
regulatory authority in connection with the Personal Data;
e) receives a request from any third party for disclosure of the Personal Data where
compliance with such request is required or purported to be required by Law; or
f) becomes aware of a Data Loss Event, and without undue delay and in any event
within 24 hours, shall notify the other Parties, by:
(i) contacting the Data Protection Officer(s) as detailed within the Schedule;
and
(ii) informing them of the Data Loss Event, including in its notification:
(A) a description of the Data Loss Event including where possible, the
categories and approximate number of Data Subjects concerned and
the categories and approximate number of data records concerned;
and
(B) a description of the measures taken or proposes to take to address
the Data Loss Event, including, where appropriate, measures to
mitigate its possible adverse effects.
11.3 Following the notification of a Data Loss Event, the informing Party shall immediately
assess the risks and consequences of the Data Loss Event and take measures,
including:
(i) to mitigate the possible adverse effects of the Data Loss Event; and
(ii) to protect the data and the interests/safety of the Data Subject and/or Data
Controller,
and inform the other Parties as to the measures that it has taken.
11.4 The informing Party’s obligation to notify shall include the provision of further
information in phases, as details become available.

Subject matter of the The PICT Service aims to empower and upskill frontline Neighbourhood
processing Housing Officers (NHOs) in the Landlord Service to support their work
with residents who may present with complex mental health difficulties
(whether diagnosed or not), and challenging behaviours, particularly
where these difficulties impact on their housing (e.g. ability to maintain
their tenancy), and vice versa.
Duration of the processing (for April 2021 until foreseeable
each Controller’s activities)
Nature and purposes of the PICT Staff offer consultation and training to NHOs, rather than usually
processing working directly with residents (although PICT staff can attend occasional
joint appointments with NHOs and residents to facilitate access to support
services). PICT staff do not have their own ‘caseload’. PICT seeks to
equip the existing workforce with the knowledge, skills and confidence to
work effectively with residents presenting with multiple and complex
mental health needs and challenging behaviours. The aim of the
intervention is to support an effective approach to achieving positive
outcomes for residents, and support the delivery of the primary task of the
Landlord Services. The hope is also that the NHOs will also develop an
increased sense of personal achievement and emotional resilience in their
work.
The project will involve PICT staff being given access to the Landlord
Work Portal, which is the electronic notes record used by NHOs to record
their contact and communication with residents
